# Sahih al-Bukhari — ingested collection

`collection.json` describes the collection; `books/001.json` … `books/097.json` hold one file per kitab, each narration validated against `CollectionBookSchema` in `src/data/schema.ts`. Everything in this folder is generated by `scripts/ingest-bukhari.ts`; do not edit the JSON by hand — fix the script or the source and re-run.

**Nothing automated here is a verdict.** The isnad/matn split and the chain are machine proposals (`split.method: "machine"`, `chain.status: "machine"`) produced by the simple, documented rules below. They exist so that a reviewer has something to correct, and so that the matn fingerprints can be compared across collections. They are not readings of the text.

## Numbering

`n` is the Muhammad Fu'ad Abd al-Baqi number, as used in Fath al-Bari and on sunnah.com, as a string. The 7,563 integers are all present. Twenty-six further narrations carry a sub-number (`"402.2"`, `"1390.3"`, …): these are the second (or third) narration printed under one number; sunnah.com shows them with a letter suffix (`sunnah.com/bukhari:402b`), fawazahmed0 encodes them as decimals, and this folder keeps the decimal form so that `parseFloat(n)` still sorts correctly.

Open-Hadith-Data's own numbering (1–7008) is not the standard one: it merges some repeated and suspended (mu'allaq) reports into the preceding row. It is kept in `sources.ohd` only for traceability.

Numbering oddities found on the way:

- fawazahmed0 ships 311 records with `reference.book: 0`. They are the records that sunnah.com prints on a combined page ("Sahih al-Bukhari 2383, 2384") or otherwise failed to tag. They were placed by the book ranges of the sunnah.com table of contents (below); the two numbers that fall *between* books in that table, 2384 and 2516, were checked on their sunnah.com pages and belong to books 42 and 48. Four numbers that the sunnah.com table of contents omits from its ranges (1066, 1772, 2286, 6860) carry a book in fawazahmed0 (16, 25, 37, 86 — the last hadith of each) and keep it. fawazahmed0's own `section_details` differ from the sunnah.com table of contents at the start of books 9, 54, 58 and 66 (521, 2711, 3156 and 4978 are the first hadith of those books); the sunnah.com table of contents was followed.
- 303 fawazahmed0 records repeat the text of the preceding record verbatim (the combined-page case above). They are aligned to the same Open-Hadith-Data row as the record before them (`shared` below) and therefore carry the same fingerprint.
- Nine fawazahmed0 records have an empty text (5710, 5711, 5712, 5774, 5775, 6074, 6075, 6174, 6175); they are gaps in the numbering and are not emitted. See "Known issues".

## Books

Book index and English names follow sunnah.com (as mirrored by fawazahmed0's `metadata.sections`). The Arabic kitab names were transcribed from the table of contents at <https://sunnah.com/bukhari> (read 2026-10-24); sunnah.com's spelling of final ya as ى in a few titles (الوحى، فى، النبى، المغازى، التمنى) was normalised to ي. Book 0 never appears in the output: see "Numbering oddities" for where its 311 records went.

## How the alignment works

Both sources list the narrations in book order, so the alignment is monotonic: no record may be aligned to a row earlier than the row of a previous record. Texts are compared after `normalizeArabic` (`src/lib/text.ts`): diacritics, tatweel, bidi marks, punctuation and quotes removed, alif variants unified, ة → ه, ى → ي, whitespace collapsed.

1. **Exact anchors.** A record whose normalised text equals an Open-Hadith-Data row within the next 80 rows is anchored to it (`exact`).
2. **Combined pages.** sunnah.com prints some consecutive numbers on one page with their texts joined ("Sahih al-Bukhari 958, 959, 960, 961"), and fawazahmed0 repeats the joined text under each number. When the joined text contains, in order, two or more free rows between the neighbouring anchors, each number of the run takes the next such row (`prefix`).
3. **Gaps.** Between consecutive anchors, the remaining records and rows are matched by an order-preserving maximum-weight matching (dynamic programming), so that a record cannot jump ahead and steal a later record's row. A pair is acceptable when one normalised text is a prefix of the other, or (both longer than 80 characters) contained in the other (`prefix`); or when the Levenshtein ratio of their first 200 normalised characters is at least **0.85** (`fuzzy`); or, failing that, when the containment of their word trigrams — shared trigrams over the trigrams of the shorter text — is at least **0.7** (`fuzzy-shingle`).
4. **Portions.** sunnah.com also numbers a *portion* of a report separately where Open-Hadith-Data keeps one row (101 is the first part of the report that 102 carries in full; 2713 is the end of the report that 2711–2712 carry whole). A still-unmatched record whose normalised text is contained in the row of its nearest matched neighbour, or in a free row between them, shares that row (`prefix`), and its `textAr` is the diacritised slice of that row corresponding to its own words, located through the index-preserving skeleton of `src/lib/isnad.ts`.
5. **Shared rows.** A record whose text repeats the previous record's verbatim shares that record's row (`shared`).
6. **Positional fill.** A record with an empty sunnah.com text would take the one row left over in its gap when the gap holds exactly as many leftover rows as empty records (`gap`). In this edition no gap qualifies: the nine empty records have no Open-Hadith-Data row either.

Anything else is `unmatched`: its `textAr` is the fawazahmed0 text (bidi marks, the `‏"‏` quote wrappers and the dash-like tatweel removed) and `sources.ohd` is absent.

Why 0.85 and 0.7. The accepted Levenshtein scores sit at 0.85–0.90: 140, 0.90–0.95: 393, 0.95–1.00: 858, exactly 1.00 (identical first 200 characters, divergence later): 693 — the differences are a variant spelling, a dropped honorific or a different tail. The best available score of the records that end up unmatched is mostly 0.20–0.60 (chains that match the neighbouring report's chain while the matn differs); the 100-odd unmatched records with a score above 0.85 are not failures of the threshold but variant portions whose row is rightly held by the neighbouring number (see "Known issues"). The Levenshtein window fails when one source expands a name in the isnad («مسلم» against «مسلم بن إبراهيم», «أبي بشر» against «أبي بشر هو جعفر بن أبي وحشية»): the 200-character windows fall out of step, and such pairs scored 0.76–0.84. Word trigrams are insensitive to insertions; their containment for the pairs accepted this way is 0.80–1.00, while unrelated neighbours score 0.00–0.55, so 0.7 sits in an empty band. The histograms are printed on every run.

## How the split works

`splitIsnadMatn` (`src/lib/isnad.ts`) builds an index-preserving, diacritic-free skeleton of the text and looks for the **last** speech marker: the Prophet's name with the eulogy carrying a verb of speech before it (قال، فقال، قالت، يقول، سمعت) or after it (قال، فقال، يقول، optionally أنه). «قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم», «أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم قال», «عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال», «قال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم» and «سمعت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يقول» all match; the bare «عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم» does not, and neither does «قالت» after the eulogy («زوج النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قالت»), which is a woman narrating. The matn begins at the first letter after the marker, mapped back to an index in `textAr`. No split is proposed when the marker ends after 85% of the text (`SPLIT_MAX_POSITION`), or when there is no marker (reports of actions, suspended reports, Companions' sayings).

Two consequences to know about:

- Taking the *last* marker means that a story in which the Prophet is quoted twice is split at the second quotation (436 texts carry more than one marker). That is a known limitation, not a bug: the proposal is meant to be reviewed.
- The position limit was first set at 70% and raised to 85% after the first run: 3,284 texts carry a marker, and in a five- or six-name isnad followed by a short matn the boundary genuinely sits at 75–90%. At 85% the split is proposed for 2,779 records (36.7%); the remaining marked texts end their marker later than that and keep a whole-text fingerprint. The limit is a single constant in `src/lib/isnad.ts`; changing it is a one-line decision for the editors.

## How the chain parse works

`parseIsnad` is applied to the isnad part (the whole text when there is no split). It tokenises the skeleton and walks it:

- transmission formulae and connectives (حدثنا، حدثني، أخبرنا، أخبرني، أنبأنا، ثنا، نا، سمعت، سمع، عن، قال، قالت، أن، أنه، يقول، قرأت على …) introduce the next name; consecutive formula words are joined into one sigha («قال حدثنا», «أنه سمع», «يقول سمعت»);
- the run of words after a formula is the name, up to the next formula, honorific (رضي الله عنه/عنها/عنهم/عنهما), closing verb (أخبره، حدثاه …) or terminator;
- the Prophet's name or eulogy, and the chain switch ح, end the chain (only the first chain of a ح-switched isnad is kept);
- the parse stops where the matn has evidently begun: a segment longer than ten words; a segment beginning with a word that cannot begin a name (كان، إذا، لما، سألت …) or containing a word that never occurs inside one (في، إلى، من، مع، ثم، حتى …); «إن» right after a verb of speech («يقول إن أناسا كانوا…», the particle, not the formula أنّ); and words between a bare verb of speech and the Prophet's name («قال غزوت مع النبي ﷺ»).

`names[i].ar` is the name as it stands in the text, diacritics kept, honorifics stripped; `sighas[i]` is its formula in skeleton spelling. The two arrays have the same length, compiler's teacher first and Companion last.

`names[i].id` is set only on an unambiguous match to a narrator record in `data/narrators/`: an alias map is built from every record's `nameAr` and `shortAr` (with أبي/أبا forms of a kunya), plus the name forms of the worked studies' isnads in `data/hadith/*.json`; an alias that resolves to more than one record is dropped. Single-word aliases from a study («سفيان» for Sufyan ibn Uyaynah in Bukhari 1) apply only to that narration: a bare «سفيان» or «يحيى» anywhere else is never linked.

## Fingerprint

`fingerprint(matn)` from `src/lib/text.ts` — the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the normalised matn — where the matn is `textAr.slice(split.at)` when there is a split, else the whole `textAr`. Bukhari 1's fingerprint equals that of the worked study `data/hadith/actions-by-intentions.json`; the script asserts it.

- **Six Open-Hadith-Data rows are unused**: 907 (sunnah.com's 960 carries this text inside the combined page 958–961, but the row spells «و أخبرني» with a detached waw and is not found verbatim), 2134 (an order difference: the text sunnah.com numbers 2297 comes after 2298's in Open-Hadith-Data, and a monotonic alignment cannot cross), 3139, 3228, 3255 and 6942 (short fragments — «على كل مسلم في كل سبعة أيام يوم…», «وبهذا الإسناد قال الله أنفق أنفق عليك» — that sunnah.com prints inside the preceding number).
- **The split is proposed for 36.7% of records.** 3,657 texts mention the Prophet without a verb of speech (actions, «كان النبي ﷺ…», «عن النبي ﷺ أنه…») and get no split; of the 3,284 with a marker, those whose marker ends after the 85% mark are refused (see above). Unsplit records have a whole-text fingerprint, which will not match the same matn in another collection.
- **The chain parse runs into the matn in some unsplit texts.** The stop rules catch most of it, but about 270 names begin with a word seen nowhere else in the chains (verbs such as «ليمنعني», «أحدثكم», «اشتكى»), i.e. they are matn words; record 4931's 24-name "chain" is its corroborations read in sequence. Names are also kept as they stand («أبيه», «أبي», «ابن شهاب»), so the same person appears under several forms; linking resolves only the twelve narrators that have records, and only on an unambiguous full form.
- **Spelling.** Open-Hadith-Data writes hamza on alif consistently («الْأَعْمَالُ») where sunnah.com often does not («الأَعْمَالُ»); `normalizeArabic` removes the difference for fingerprints, but `textAr` keeps each source's typing. Both are NFC-normalised. The Arabic book names normalise sunnah.com's final ى to ي.
- **Numbering.** 311 fawazahmed0 records with `book: 0` were placed as described under "Numbering"; the sub-numbers keep the decimal form (`"402.2"`) rather than sunnah.com's letter form (`402b`).

## Re-running

```sh
pnpm tsx scripts/ingest-bukhari.ts   # downloads sources/bukhari/* when absent, rewrites this folder's JSON
pnpm check:data                       # validates everything under /data
```

The script fails loudly if the schema, the unit-style assertions on Bukhari 1, 2 and 7563, or the monotonicity of the alignment are violated.
