# Sahih al-Bukhari 6

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 2" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Hadith 2 Notes", ID `13SPUNsYnbGS-lOm_U1rQhnv1aqKfHbBELf323vJollQ`). The notes give no Fath al-Bari number. [added] Identified as al-Bukhari **6** (Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering; Badʾ al-Waḥy) from the chain and chapter stated in the notes.

## Text
- Arabic matn: only the fragment «كان أجود ما يكون» appears in the notes. [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:6] كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَجْوَدَ النَّاسِ، وَكَانَ أَجْوَدُ مَا يَكُونُ فِي رَمَضَانَ حِينَ يَلْقَاهُ جِبْرِيلُ، وَكَانَ يَلْقَاهُ فِي كُلِّ لَيْلَةٍ مِنْ رَمَضَانَ فَيُدَارِسُهُ الْقُرْآنَ، فَلَرَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَجْوَدُ بِالْخَيْرِ مِنَ الرِّيحِ الْمُرْسَلَةِ
- English as rendered in the notes: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was at his most generous in Ramadan when Jibrīl met him. Jibrīl would meet him every night in Ramadan and revise the Qurʾān with him. Indeed, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was more generous in goodness than the blowing wind."
- Gist: Ibn ʿAbbās reports that the Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and most generous of all in Ramadan when Jibrīl met him every night to review the Qurʾān.
- Kitab / bab: "the chapter on the Beginning of Revelation to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ" (Badʾ al-Waḥy)

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
Two chains joined by ح (chain switch), converging at al-Zuhrī. The notes diagram it as: "ʿAbdān narrated to us; he said: ʿAbdullāh informed us; he said: Yūnus informed us from al-Zuhrī. ح And Bishr ibn Muḥammad narrated to us; he said: ʿAbdullāh informed us; he said: Yūnus and Maʿmar informed us from al-Zuhrī with a similar wording. He said: ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh informed me from Ibn ʿAbbās."

Chain A
1. ʿAbdān | ḥaddathanā ("narrated to us") | laqab of ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUthmān; Marwazī
2. ʿAbdullāh [ibn al-Mubārak] | qāla akhbaranā ("informed us") | Marwazī
3. Yūnus [ibn Yazīd] | qāla akhbaranā | —
4. al-Zuhrī | ʿan | ح switch here

Chain B
1. Bishr ibn Muḥammad | ḥaddathanā | Marwazī; al-Bukhārī alone of the six uses him
2. ʿAbdullāh [ibn al-Mubārak] | qāla akhbaranā | —
3. Yūnus and Maʿmar | qāla akhbaranā ... ʿan al-Zuhrī *naḥwahu* ("with a similar narration") | wording is Yūnus's, Maʿmar's conveys the meaning (Fatḥ al-Bārī). The notes also render this link as "Yūnus informed us — and Maʿmar from al-Zuhrī with a similar narration"
4. al-Zuhrī | qāla akhbaranī ("informed me") | —

Common tail
5. ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh [ibn ʿUtbah ibn Masʿūd] | ʿan | one of the Seven Jurists
6. Ibn ʿAbbās | qāla | Companion; marfūʿ fiʿlī (by action)

## Narrators

### ʿAbdān (ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUthmān)
- Full name and nasab: ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUthmān ibn Jabalah ibn Abī Rawwād — Abī Rawwād's name was Maymūn, also said Ayman — al-Azdī al-ʿAtakī (Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb)
- Kunya: Abū ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān
- Laqab / title: ʿAbdān (laqab); al-ḥāfiẕ
- Nisba(s): al-Azdī al-ʿAtakī; al-Marwazī
- Mawla status: "their client (mawlā)" — of al-Azd / al-ʿAtīk
- City / region: Marw
- Tabaqa: tenth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Reliable, ḥāfiẕ, from the tenth generation. He died in the year 221 AH, in Shaʿbān"
- Critics' statements:
  - Aḥmad ibn ʿAbdah — gave a million dirhams in charity in his life; copied Ibn al-Mubārak's books with a single pen — (quoted by Ibn Ḥajar, Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb)
  - Abū Rajāʾ Muḥammad ibn Ḥamdawayh — declared him reliable — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - al-Ḥākim — "the Imām of the people of ḥadīth in his land" — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
- Teachers mentioned: Shuʿbah, Mālik, Ibn al-Mubārak (al-Khulāṣah)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhārī, adh-Dhuhlī, many others (al-Khulāṣah)
- Died: Shaʿbān 221 AH
- Rumuz: all the major compilers except Ibn Mājah

### Bishr ibn Muḥammad
- Full name and nasab: Bishr ibn Muḥammad as-Sakhtiyānī
- Kunya: Abū Muḥammad (= father's name)
- Nisba(s): as-Sakhtiyānī; al-Marwazī
- City / region: Marw
- Tabaqa: not stated
- Grading (al-Khulāṣah, as rendered): truthful (ṣadūq), though accused of irjāʾ
- Critics' statements: Ibn Ḥibbān — included him among the reliable (ath-Thiqāt) — (al-Khulāṣah context)
- Teachers mentioned: Ibn al-Mubārak
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhārī
- Died: 224 AH
- Rumuz: al-Bukhārī only, "not of the remaining authors of the Six Books"

### ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak
- Full name and nasab: ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak ibn Wāḍiḥ al-Ḥanẕalī at-Tamīmī (Tahdhīb)
- Kunya: Abū ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān
- Laqab / title: "one of the leading Imāms"
- Nisba(s): al-Ḥanẕalī at-Tamīmī; al-Marwazī
- Mawla status: "their client" (of Banū Ḥanẕalah / Tamīm)
- City / region: Marw
- Tabaqa: eighth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Reliable, firm, jurist, scholar, generous, mujāhid; all good qualities were gathered in him. From the eighth generation. He died in 181 AH at the age of sixty-three"
- Critics' statements: Ibn ʿUyaynah — "I examined the deeds of the Companions and saw no superiority they had over Ibn al-Mubārak except their companionship of the Prophet ﷺ and fighting alongside him" — (Tahdhīb context)
- Teachers mentioned: Maʿmar ibn Rāshid, Yūnus ibn Yazīd, many others (al-Maqdisī, al-Jamʿ bayna Rijāl aṣ-Ṣaḥīḥayn)
- Students mentioned: ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Mahdī, Muslim ibn Ibrāhīm, others (al-Maqdisī)
- Died: 181 AH, aged 63
- Rumuz: all six books

### Maʿmar ibn Rāshid
- Full name and nasab: Maʿmar ibn Rāshid al-Azdī
- Kunya: Abū ʿUrwah
- Nisba(s): al-Azdī; al-Baṣrī, "who settled in Yemen"
- Mawla status: "their client" (of al-Azd)
- City / region: Basra, then Yemen
- Tabaqa: seventh (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Reliable, firm, virtuous, except that there is some weakness in his narrations from Thābit, al-Aʿmash and Hishām ibn ʿUrwah, and also in what he narrated in Basrah"
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Ḥajar — "Maʿmar, the companion of al-Zuhrī, was among the most reliable narrators from him" — Fatḥ al-Bārī
  - Ibn Maʿīn, al-ʿIjlī, Yaʿqūb ibn Shaybah, an-Nasāʾī and others — reliable — (book not named)
- Notable: al-Bukhārī narrated from him only via his narrations from al-Zuhrī, Ibn Ṭāwūs, Hammām ibn Munabbih, Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr, Hishām ibn ʿUrwah, Ayyūb, Thumāmah ibn Anas, ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Jazarī and others, and avoided his Basran students except where supported
- Died: 154 AH, aged 58
- Rumuz: all six books

### Yūnus ibn Yazīd al-Aylī and Ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī

### ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh
- Full name and nasab: ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUtbah ibn Masʿūd al-Hudhalī (al-Maqdisī)
- Kunya: Abū ʿAbdullāh (= father's name)
- Laqab / title: one of the Seven Jurists of Madinah (al-fuqahāʾ as-sabʿah)
- Nisba(s): al-Hudhalī; "an ally of Banī Zuhrah"
- Mawla status: ally (ḥalīf), not mawlā
- City / region: Madinah
- Tabaqa: third (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Reliable, jurist, firm, from the third generation. He died in 94 AH — or 98 — and other dates are mentioned"
- Critics' statements: "Many Imāms praised and authenticated him" (unnamed)
- Teachers mentioned (both Ṣaḥīḥs): Ibn ʿAbbās, Zayd ibn Khālid, Abū Hurayrah, Umm Qays bint Miḥṣan, ʿĀʾishah, Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī; "many others in Muslim" (al-Maqdisī)
- Students mentioned: al-Zuhrī (from the chain)
- Died: 94 or 98 AH, other dates mentioned
- Rumuz: all six books

### ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās
- Full name and nasab: ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib al-Hāshimī (al-Khulāṣah)
- Kunya: Abū al-ʿAbbās (= father's name)
- Laqab / title: cousin of the Prophet ﷺ; "the scholar of the Ummah", "its jurist", "the Interpreter of the Qurʾān"; called "the sea" (al-baḥr) and "the scholar" (al-ḥibr) for his knowledge; one of the mukthirūn; one of the four ʿAbādilah
- Nisba(s): al-Hāshimī (Qurashī)
- Tabaqa: Companion
- Taqrīb (as rendered): born three years before the Hijrah; the Prophet ﷺ prayed for him to have understanding of the Qurʾān; died 68 AH in aṭ-Ṭāʾif
- Hadith counts (al-Khulāṣah): 1,660 total; 75 agreed; 28 al-Bukhārī only; 49 Muslim only; 25 heard directly from the Prophet ﷺ, the rest from Companions. Number in al-Bukhārī: 217 (Ibn Ḥajar, introduction to Fatḥ al-Bārī)
- Students mentioned (both Ṣaḥīḥs): Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUtbah, al-Qāsim ibn Muḥammad ibn Abī Bakr, many Tābiʿīn (al-Maqdisī)
- Died: 68 AH, aṭ-Ṭāʾif
- Rumuz: all six books
- Notable: Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb rejects the claims that he heard only nine (or ten, or four) hadith directly — the two Ṣaḥīḥs alone have more than ten with explicit hearing, plus observed actions, plus more outside them

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All eight narrators of the two chains are in the six books except ʿAbdān (not Ibn Mājah) and Bishr ibn Muḥammad (al-Bukhārī only). [computable]
2. Two Madinan Tābiʿīs — al-Zuhrī and his shaykh ʿUbaydullāh — so a Tābiʿī narrating from a Tābiʿī. [computable]
3. Three narrators from Marw: ʿAbdān, Bishr, and their common shaykh ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak. [computable]
4. Two mawālī: ʿAbdān and Maʿmar ibn Rāshid (so the notes; Ibn al-Mubārak's biography also says "their client"). [computable]
5. ح marks the switch between two chains, to avoid repeating names; both converge at al-Zuhrī. Al-Bukhārī did not merge them earlier at Ibn al-Mubārak because ʿAbdān has Ibn al-Mubārak ← Yūnus alone, while Bishr has Ibn al-Mubārak ← Yūnus and Maʿmar together. [computable from chain structure]
6. *Naḥwahu*: the wording is Yūnus's, Maʿmar's narration conveys the meaning (Fatḥ al-Bārī); Ibn Ḥajar's observation (on the Jābir hadith in Tayammum) that al-Bukhārī's wording is usually that of the last narrator mentioned, so the matn follows Bishr ibn Muḥammad's wording. [scholar]
7. ʿUbaydullāh is one of the Seven Jurists of Madinah: six agreed (Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, ʿUrwah ibn az-Zubayr, al-Qāsim ibn Muḥammad, Khārijah ibn Zayd, ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh, Sulaymān ibn Yasār); the seventh disputed — Abū Salamah ibn ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān (al-Ḥākim), Sālim ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar (Ibn al-Mubārak), or Abū Bakr ibn ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn al-Ḥārith (Abū az-Zinād) — an-Nawawī, al-Ishārāt ilā Asmāʾ al-Mubhamāt; Ibn al-Qayyim, Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn, with the mnemonic verse «إذا قيل: من في العلم سبعة أبحر * روايتهم ليست عن العلم خارجه / فقل: هم عبيد الله، عروة، قاسم * سعيد، أبو بكر، سليمان، خارجة». [computable as a tag; membership is scholar data]
8. Ibn ʿAbbās is one of the ʿAbādilah — of 220 Companions named ʿAbdullāh the title is used for four: Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn az-Zubayr, Ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (al-ʿIrāqī, al-Mustafād). [computable as a tag]
9. ʿAbdān is a laqab (name ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUthmān); al-Zuhrī is known both by clan name and by attribution to his grandfather (Ibn Shihāb). [computable]
10. Kunya matching the father's name (Nukhbat al-Fikar): four here — ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās (Abū al-ʿAbbās), ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿAbdullāh (Abū ʿAbdullāh), Yūnus ibn Yazīd (Abū Yazīd), Bishr ibn Muḥammad (Abū Muḥammad). [computable]
11. The matn is marfūʿ fiʿlī (by action), explicitly. [scholar]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - أجود الناس — the person of greatest generosity; generous with his self (tooth broken, face wounded), his status, his wealth, and in guiding and teaching
  - أجود — nominative in most narrations (predicate of kāna with its own predicate omitted, or a new subject with "in Ramadan" as predicate), accusative in some (predicate of kāna with a pronoun subject): "during Ramadan he was more generous than at other times"
  - "The Messenger was the most generous of people" first, then Ramadan — an iḥtirās (precaution) so no one thinks his generosity was limited to Ramadan
  - الريح المرسلة — the wind of mercy sent with widespread rain (Qurʾān: "He sends the winds as glad tidings..."; "Allah is the One who sends the winds, stirring up clouds"); "sent forth" (1) for the universality of his benefit, (2) to distinguish it from the barren harmful wind
  - "more generous *in goodness*" — بالخير placed before the comparison so it is not misread as qualifying "the wind sent forth"
  - يدارسه — mutual study, from both sides: the Prophet presenting (Ibn ʿAbbās, Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān) and Jibrīl reviewing (Abū Hurayrah: once each year, twice in the year he died)
- Commentaries cited: Fatḥ al-Bārī — on *naḥwahu* and whose wording is used; on why the hadith is placed in Badʾ al-Waḥy (the Qurʾān's descent to the lowest heaven in one piece in Ramadan, per Ibn ʿAbbās; annual review connects it to revelation). Wisdom of increased generosity: the virtue of the time, of what was revealed, of the one who brought it, and of the review; ʿĀʾishah: "his character was the Qurʾān".
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Allah's favour on His Prophet ﷺ in making him the most generous of people.
  2. Encouragement to generosity at all times.
  3. Encouragement to increase generosity in Ramadan.
  4. Desirability of increased Qurʾān recitation in Ramadan; indication of its superiority over other forms of remembrance.
  5. Desirability of visiting righteous people.
  6. Permissibility of repeated visits when not disliked.
  7. Permissibility of saying "Ramadan" without "the month of".
  8. Use of analogies and parables to clarify meanings.
  9. Jibrīl recited the Qurʾān outside the time of revelation.
  10. "The Qurʾān" may refer to a portion of it.
  11. Desirability of mutual study (tadārus) of the Qurʾān.
  12. Continual recitation leads to increase in goodness.
  13. A superior person may revisit knowledge he already knows, for reminder and benefit.
  14. The virtue of a special time is attained by increasing worship within it.
  15. The greatest fruit of recitation is its effect on conduct ("his character was the Qurʾān"; "the Qurʾān is a proof for you or against you").
  16. The exalted status of Ramadan, month of the Qurʾān's revelation and of Jibrīl's frequent descent.
  17. Belief in Jibrīl's descent, ascent and study with the Prophet ﷺ is part of belief in the angels, one of the six pillars of īmān (hadith of Jibrīl).
