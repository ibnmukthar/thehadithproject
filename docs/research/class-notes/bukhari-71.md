# Sahih al-Bukhari 71

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 1" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Additional Notes to Hadith 1 of Study of Sahih al-Bukhari", ID `1QdUBbJxD7YZMJcMlpRqRaK6WkUKpyezdf538HJDj59g`). The notes give no Fath al-Bari number. [added] Identified as al-Bukhari **71** (Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering; Kitab al-ʿIlm, bab "من يرد الله به خيرا يفقهه في الدين") from the chain (Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr → Ibn Wahb → Yūnus → Ibn Shihāb → Ḥumayd → Muʿāwiyah) and the three sentences of the matn discussed in the notes.
>
> **Coverage note:** this document is a continuation. It begins mid-way through the narrator biographies ("Third: Yūnus ibn Yazīd") and so does **not** contain the matn, the takhrij section, or the biographies of narrators 1 (Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr) and 2 (ʿAbd Allāh ibn Wahb). Everything recorded below about those parts is only what the later sections mention in passing.

## Text
- Arabic matn: not given in this document. [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:71] مَنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ بِهِ خَيْرًا يُفَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّينِ، وَإِنَّمَا أَنَا قَاسِمٌ وَاللَّهُ يُعْطِي، وَلَنْ تَزَالَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةُ قَائِمَةً عَلَى أَمْرِ اللَّهِ لاَ يَضُرُّهُمْ مَنْ خَالَفَهُمْ حَتَّى يَأْتِيَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ
- The notes treat the matn as three independent statements: (1) "Whoever Allah intends good for, He gives him understanding of the religion"; (2) "I am only a distributor, while Allah gives"; (3) "there will always remain a group of this Ummah upholding Allah's command; those who oppose them will not harm them until Allah's Decree comes".
- Gist: Muʿāwiyah, while preaching, reports the Prophet ﷺ saying that understanding of the religion is a sign of Allah's good intent for a person, that the Prophet only apportions what Allah gives, and that a group of this Ummah will remain upholding Allah's command until His decree comes.
- Kitab / bab: not stated in this document (the notes only say the first sentence "corresponds to the chapter heading"). [added] Kitab al-ʿIlm, bab man yurid Allah bihi khayran yufaqqihhu fi al-din.

## Narrators

### Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr
- Biography not in this document. From sections three–four only: al-Bukhārī's shaykh; Egyptian; mawlā of the Anṣār; narrated from by al-Bukhārī, Muslim, an-Nasāʾī and Abū Dāwūd (in *al-Qadr*), "but not by the remaining compilers" of the six.

### ʿAbd Allāh ibn Wahb
- Biography not in this document. From section three only: Egyptian; mawlā of the Qurashīs; a mudallis who, in this hadith, explicitly said *akhbaranā* / *anbanā*; Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Uways and Ḥarmalah ibn Yaḥyā are his other students for this hadith.

### Yūnus ibn Yazīd al-Aylī
- Full name and nasab: Yūnus ibn Yazīd ibn Abī an-Najjād al-Aylī (al-Aylī: "with the hamzah open, the yāʾ still, followed by a lām")
- Kunya: Abū Yazīd
- Laqab / title: —
- Nisba(s): al-Aylī
- Mawla status: freed slave (mawlā) of Muʿāwiyah ibn Abī Sufyān (Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb); the lata'if call him "the freedman of the Umayyads"
- City / region: not stated beyond the nisba
- Tabaqa: not stated
- Taqrīb line (as rendered in notes): trustworthy, though some minor errors in his narrations from az-Zuhrī and a few mistakes in narrations from others
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Ḥajar — the majority declared him trustworthy without qualification; criticised only where he differed from his peers or narrated from memory; sound when narrating from his written notes — Hady as-Sārī
  - ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī (reported by Ibn al-Barqī) — the most reliable from az-Zuhrī are Mālik, Ibn ʿUyaynah, Maʿmar, Ziyād ibn Saʿd, and Yūnus when narrating from his book — (book not named; in the Hady as-Sārī / Tahdhīb context)
  - Aḥmad, Ibn Maʿīn, al-ʿAjlī, an-Nasāʾī, Yaʿqūb ibn Shaybah "and the majority" — trustworthy — (book not named)
- Teachers mentioned: az-Zuhrī; Nāfiʿ (freed slave of Ibn ʿUmar); Hishām ibn ʿUrwah (Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb)
- Students mentioned: al-Layth; al-Awzāʿī; Ibn al-Mubārak; Ibn Wahb (Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb)
- Died: 159 AH — "the correct view" (Taqrīb); others said 160 AH
- Rumuz: "used by all the major compilers"
- Notable: kunya identical to his father's name (Abū Yazīd ibn Yazīd) — a Nukhbat al-Fikar category

### Ibn Shihāb az-Zuhrī
- Full name and nasab: Muḥammad ibn Muslim ibn ʿUbayd Allāh ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shihāb ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Ḥārith ibn Zuhrah ibn Kilāb al-Qurashī az-Zuhrī
- Kunya: Abū Bakr
- Laqab / title: the jurist (al-faqīh), the ḥāfiẕ; known both as az-Zuhrī (clan) and Ibn Shihāb (attribution to his ancestor)
- Nisba(s): al-Qurashī, az-Zuhrī
- Mawla status: — (Qurashī by lineage)
- City / region: Madinah ("the ḥadīth memoriser of Madinah, one of the leading imāms and scholars of both Ḥijāz and Shām")
- Tabaqa: not stated (a Tābiʿī; "met ten Companions" — Ibn Khallikān)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): a jurist, a ḥadīth memoriser, unanimously recognised for his prestige and precision
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Ḥajar — "the jurist, Abū Bakr, the ḥadīth memoriser of Madinah, one of the leading imāms and scholars of both Ḥijāz and Shām" — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī (reported by al-Bukhārī) — az-Zuhrī had about two thousand hadith — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - Ibn Saʿd — trustworthy, abundant in hadith, knowledge and transmission; a jurist and comprehensive scholar — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - Ibn Shihāb himself (Ibn Wahb ← al-Layth) — "I have never placed anything in my heart and forgotten it" — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - an-Nasāʾī — the best chains from the Prophet ﷺ are four, including az-Zuhrī ← ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn ← his father ← his grandfather, and az-Zuhrī ← ʿUbayd Allāh ← Ibn ʿAbbās — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb
  - Makḥūl — asked who was the most knowledgeable person he had seen, answered "Ibn Shihāb" three times — (Ibn Khallikān, Wafayāt al-Aʿyān context)
  - adh-Dhahabī — one who rarely practised tadlīs — Mīzān al-Iʿtidāl
- Teachers mentioned: Companions including ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, Anas ibn Mālik, Abū aṭ-Ṭufayl, Maḥmūd ibn ar-Rabīʿ; the seven jurists of Madinah; others
- Students mentioned: ʿAṭāʾ ibn Abī Rabāḥ, Abū az-Zubayr al-Makkī, al-Awzāʿī, Ibn Jurayj, Yūnus ibn Yazīd, al-Layth ibn Saʿd, "Hishīm" (so in notes), Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah (Tahdhīb); Mālik ibn Anas, Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, Sufyān ath-Thawrī (Ibn Khallikān)
- Born / died: born 51 AH ("it was said"); died the night of Tuesday 17 Ramaḍān 124 AH — also said 123 or 125 — aged 72 or 73
- Rumuz: "included by all the major ḥadīth compilers"
- Notable: undertook the recording of hadith by order of ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, hence called the founder of hadith compilation; as-Suyūṭī's Alfiyyah couplet ("The first to compile ḥadīth and reports — was Ibn Shihāb, commanded by ʿUmar") cited; memorised the knowledge of the seven jurists; anecdote of surrounding himself with books (his wife: "harder for me to bear than three co-wives")

- Mawla status: — (Qurashī)
- City / region: Madinah
- Tabaqa: second (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Trustworthy, from the second generation" (ثقة من الثانية)

  - al-ʿAjlī, Abū Zurʿah, Abū Khirāsh — trustworthy — Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb

### Muʿāwiyah ibn Abī Sufyān
- Full name and nasab: Muʿāwiyah ibn Abī Sufyān Ṣakhr ibn Ḥarb ibn Umayyah al-Umawī
- Kunya: Abū ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān
- Laqab / title: the Caliph; "uncle of the believers, scribe of Allah's Revelation, one of the caliphs of the Muslims" (Ibn Qudāmah, Lumʿat al-Iʿtiqād); "the first of the Muslim kings and the best of them" (the commentator on al-ʿAqīdah aṭ-Ṭaḥāwiyyah)
- Nisba(s): al-Umawī (Qurashī)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Companion who accepted Islam before the conquest of Makkah, served as a scribe of revelation, and died in Rajab of the year 60 AH at nearly eighty years of age"
- Islam: on the day of the Conquest, "and some said before it" (Tahdhīb at-Tahdhīb)
- Narrated from: the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, his sister Umm Ḥabībah
- Students mentioned: Jarīr ibn ʿAbd Allāh al-Bajalī, as-Sāʾib ibn Yazīd al-Kindī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, Qays ibn Abī Ḥāzim, Ḥumayd ibn ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, many others
- Career: appointed governor of Shām by ʿUmar after his brother Yazīd; kept in post by ʿUthmān; then caliph; Ibn Isḥāq: "governor for twenty years and a caliph for twenty years"
- Hadith counts (al-Khulāṣah): 130 total; 4 agreed; 4 al-Bukhārī only; 5 Muslim only. Number in al-Bukhārī: not stated.
- Died: Rajab 60 AH
- Rumuz: in al-Bukhārī and Muslim and the rest of the six (lata'if point 1)
- Notable: the notes add a long excursus on the dispute with ʿAlī (both exercised ijtihād; the correct one has two rewards, the mistaken one reward and is forgiven) and the obligation to speak only good of the Companions, citing: al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah and al-Ḥashr 59:10; the hadith "Do not revile my Companions..."; aṭ-Ṭaḥāwī's ʿAqīdah and its commentator; Ibn Kathīr's al-Bidāyah wan-Nihāyah (Aḥmad via al-Bayhaqī on the four caliphs — "May Allah have mercy on Muʿāwiyah"; Ibn Abī ad-Dunyā's report of ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz's dream); Ibn ʿAsākir (Abū Zurʿah ar-Rāzī's rebuke of a man who hated Muʿāwiyah); Aḥmad reciting al-Baqarah 2:134; al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī via al-Awzāʿī; Ibn al-Mubārak (Muʿāwiyah's "Rabbanā laka l-ḥamd" behind the Prophet; the dust in his horse's nostrils better than ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz); al-Muʿāfā ibn ʿImrān; Abū Tūbah ar-Rabīʿ ibn Nāfiʿ al-Ḥalabī ("Muʿāwiyah was a veil for the Companions"); al-Maymūnī and al-Faḍl ibn Ziyād from Aḥmad; Ibrāhīm ibn Maysarah (ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz whipped only a man who insulted Muʿāwiyah).

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All six narrators are in both al-Bukhārī and Muslim, and in the rest of the six books too, except Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, an-Nasāʾī, Abū Dāwūd in *al-Qadr* only). [computable]
2. Two Madinan Tābiʿīs, both Qurashī and both of Banū Zuhrah — Ibn Shihāb and his teacher Ḥumayd — so a Tābiʿī narrating from a Tābiʿī. [computable]
3. Two Egyptians: Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr and Ibn Wahb. [computable]
4. The upper half of the chain (Muʿāwiyah, Ḥumayd, Ibn Shihāb) are Qurashīs; the lower half (Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr, mawlā of the Anṣār; Ibn Wahb, mawlā of the Qurashīs; Yūnus, mawlā of the Umayyads) are mawālī. [computable]
5. Yūnus was the freedman of Muʿāwiyah, so patron and freedman both appear in one chain (notes' wording: "a freedman at its top (Muʿāwiyah) and a freedman at its bottom (Yūnus)"). [computable]
6. Muʿāwiyah's "I heard the Prophet ﷺ say" makes the matn marfūʿ qawlī taṣrīḥan (مرفوع قولي تصريحًا). [computable from formula]
7. Ḥumayd's description of the setting (Muʿāwiyah delivering a sermon) is used by hadith scholars as evidence of the narrator's precision in receiving and transmitting. [scholar]
8. Ibn Wahb is a mudallis but states *akhbaranā* / *anbanā*; Ibn Shihāb's *qāla* here functions like *ʿan*, but he says *akhbaranā* in Kitāb al-Iʿtiṣām and *ḥaddathanā* in Muslim's Zakāh; adh-Dhahabī says he rarely did tadlīs. [computable: tadlīs flag and formula; resolving it via other routes is scholar]
9. Iʿtibār: Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Uways (al-Bukhārī) and Ḥarmalah ibn Yaḥyā (Muslim), both from Ibn Wahb, are a complete mutābaʿah of Saʿīd ibn ʿUfayr; Ḥibbān ibn Mūsā ← Ibn al-Mubārak ← Yūnus and similar are partial mutābaʿāt; the hadiths of ʿUmar, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and Abū Hurayrah are shawāhid. [computable from route graph; classification is scholar]
10. Disputed / multiple kunya (Nukhbat al-Fikar category): Ḥumayd — Abū Ibrāhīm, Abū ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān or Abū ʿUthmān. [computable]
11. Kunya matching the father's name (Nukhbat al-Fikar category): Yūnus, Abū Yazīd ibn Yazīd. [computable]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - يفقهه — "makes him comprehend"; *faquha* (ḍamma) when fiqh becomes second nature; *faqiha* (fatḥa) when one outstrips others in comprehension; *faqiha* (kasra) when one understands; intended here: understanding of sharʿī rulings that bears fruit in action
  - قاسم — distributor: "I do not give anyone or withhold from anyone except by Allah's command"
  - أمر الله — two senses in the last sentence: "upholding Allah's command" = His law and religion (cf. "whoever introduces into this matter of ours..."); "until Allah's command comes" = His decree, namely the pleasant wind at the end of time that takes every believer's soul (Muslim, Kitāb al-Fitan)
  - "this Ummah" — ummat ad-daʿwah (general: all humans and jinn since the sending of the Prophet) vs ummat al-ijābah (specific: those who responded); here general phrasing with a specific meaning (some of the Ummah), as shown by al-Mughīrah's "a group (ṭāʾifah) of my Ummah"
  - الطائفة المنصورة — al-Bukhārī: the people of knowledge of the traditions; Aḥmad: "if they are not the people of ḥadīth, I do not know who they are"; Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ: the people of the Sunnah who hold the creed of the hadith people; an-Nawawī: any faction upholding Allah's command (mujāhid, jurist, hadith scholar, ascetic, one who commands right and forbids wrong), not necessarily in one place — all cited via Fatḥ al-Bārī
- Commentaries cited: Fatḥ al-Bārī — all three sentences relate to the chapter heading; the implication that one who does not learn the principles of Islam is deprived of good; Abū Yaʿlā's weak addition; the statements on the Victorious Group. Al-Bukhārī's placements (Khums, Iʿtiṣām, Manāqib) are read as showing his purposes.
- Creed excursus: affirming Allah's will (irādah) on the Ahl as-Sunnah method (ash-Shūrā 42:11, affirm what He affirmed, no takyīf, tamthīl, taʾwīl or taʿṭīl; what is said of some attributes is said of all); two kinds of will — kawniyyah qadariyyah (universal, covers what He loves and dislikes, must occur) and dīniyyah sharʿiyyah (what He loves, need not occur) — with examples (al-Anʿām 6:149; as-Sajdah 32:13; al-Balad 90:8–10; Yūnus 10:25 — object of "invites" omitted for generality, object of "guides" expressed for specificity; at-Takwīr 81:29; al-Baqarah 2:185; an-Nisāʾ 4:27; Hūd 11:34; al-Baqarah 2:253); two ways of knowing what Allah has decreed — its occurrence, or the report of the Prophet ﷺ; decree does not strip the servant of will (voluntary vs involuntary movements); "command", like "will", has a kawnī and a sharʿī sense, and this hadith shows both.
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Encouragement to seek knowledge.
  2. Virtue of the scholars over others.
  3. Virtue of deep understanding of the religion over other sciences.
  4. Affirming the attribute of will for Allah.
  5. Fiqh in the religion is a sign that Allah intends good for a servant.
  6. Guiding people to the Sunnah and proclaiming it from the pulpits.
  7. Belief in qaḍāʾ and qadar; the Giver and Withholder is Allah alone.
  8. Glad tidings that goodness remains in this Ummah.
  9. The Prophet ﷺ informed of what is to come, from the unseen Allah taught him.
  10. This foretelling is among the signs of his prophethood, fulfilled over fourteen centuries.
  11. Obligation to believe the truth continues until Allah's decree comes.
  12. Prophetic foretelling is one of the two ways of knowing what Allah has decreed; the other is occurrence.
  13. The Victorious Group has opponents and adversaries.
  14. Exhortation to hold fast to the Book and Sunnah so as to be of this group.
  15. Its enemies will not harm it or stop the continuation of the truth.
  16. The continuation of the truth within the Ummah is a tremendous virtue for it.
  17. Consensus (ijmāʿ) is a proof.
