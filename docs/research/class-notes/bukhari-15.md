## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: لاَ يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى أَكُونَ أَحَبَّ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ وَالِدِهِ وَوَلَدِهِ وَالنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ
- English gist: None of you has complete faith until the Prophet ﷺ is dearer to him than his father, his child and all people.
- Kitab / bab: Book of Faith, chapter "Love for the Messenger ﷺ Is Part of Faith"; placed immediately after Abu Hurayrah's hadith "By the One in Whose Hand is my soul, none of you truly believes until I am more beloved to him than his father and his child".

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
First chain:
1. al-Bukhari | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim | al-Dawraqi; Baghdadi; teacher of all six
2. Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Ibn 'Ulayyah | = Isma'il ibn Ibrahim ibn Miqsam; attributed to his mother
3. Ibn 'Ulayyah | "from" (عن) → 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Suhayb | 
4. 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Suhayb | "from" (عن) → Anas | 
5. Anas | "from the Prophet ﷺ" (عن النبي ﷺ) | 

ح (second chain, joined after the first is completed to Anas):
1. al-Bukhari | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Adam | = Adam ibn Abi Iyas; from 'Asqalan, raised in Baghdad
2. Adam | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Shu'bah | = Shu'bah ibn al-Hajjaj
3. Shu'bah | "from" (عن) → Qatadah | Qatadah is a mudallis; Shu'bah narrates only what Qatadah heard; explicit hearing in al-Nasa'i's route ("who heard Anas")
4. Qatadah | "from" (عن) → Anas | 
5. Anas | "The Prophet ﷺ said" (قال النبي ﷺ) | 

The quoted wording is Qatadah's. Four narrators between al-Bukhari and the Prophet ﷺ on each chain.

## Narrators

### Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim al-Dawraqi
- Full name and nasab: Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim ibn Kathir ibn Aflah al-'Abdi
- Kunya: Abu Yusuf
- Laqab / title: al-Dawraqi — from the caps (dawraqiyyah) they wore, not from the town of Dawraq (al-Maqdisi)
- Nisba(s): al-'Abdi, al-Qaysi (by clientage); al-Dawraqi
- Mawla status: "their client", client of 'Abd al-Qays
- City / region: Baghdad ("the Baghdad-based hadith master")
- Tabaqa: tenth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy ... one of the hadith masters" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: al-Khatib — "a precise hadith master who compiled a Musnad"
- Teachers mentioned: Yahya ibn Abi Za'idah, Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Abi Hazim, many others (al-Khulasah)
- Students mentioned: all six compilers (al-Khulasah)
- Born / died: born 166 AH, two years before his brother Ahmad (Abu al-'Abbas al-Sarraj, via al-Maqdisi); died 252 AH aged 96 (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: brother of Ahmad [ibn Ibrahim al-Dawraqi]; died four years before al-Bukhari; a direct teacher of all six compilers.

### Ibn 'Ulayyah
- Full name and nasab: Isma'il ibn Ibrahim ibn Miqsam al-Asadi
- Kunya: Abu Bishr
- Laqab / title: Ibn 'Ulayyah — 'Ulayyah was his mother's name (a client of Banu Asad); he disliked it: "Whoever calls me Ibn 'Ulayyah has backbitten me"; al-Shafi'i would say "Isma'il, who is called Ibn 'Ulayyah, informed us"
- Nisba(s): al-Asadi (by clientage); al-Basri
- Mawla status: "their client", client of Banu Asad ibn Khuzaymah through both father and mother
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: eighth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "a trustworthy hadith master" [ثقة حافظ, added]
- Critics' statements: Shu'bah — "Isma'il ibn 'Ulayyah is the fragrant flower of the jurists"; "Ibn 'Ulayyah is the master of the hadith scholars" — Tahdhib; Ahmad — "the foremost authority in Basra in careful verification" — Tahdhib; Ibn al-Madini — "I would regard Ibn 'Ulayyah as unsurpassed in precision in hadith" — Tahdhib; al-Dhahabi — "a hadith master and jurist of great standing" — al-Mizan
- Born / died: died 193 AH aged 83 (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: attribution to the mother (see subtleties); superlative-grade ta'dil.

### 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Suhayb
- Full name and nasab: 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Suhayb al-Bunani (ba' and two nuns)
- Nisba(s): al-Bunani (by clientage); al-Basri
- Mawla status: client of Bunanah ibn Sa'd ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib (al-Khulasah); "al-Bunani, their client" (Tahdhib)
- City / region: Basra; "the blind Basran narrator"
- Tabaqa: fourth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in, Ibn Sa'd, al-Nasa'i, al-'Ijli — affirmed his reliability — Tahdhib
- Teachers mentioned: Anas, Shahr (al-Khulasah)
- Students mentioned: Shu'bah, the two Hammads (al-Khulasah)
- Born / died: died 130 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six

### Anas ibn Malik (Companion)
- The Companion of both chains; biography "presented in the discussion of the narrators of the sixth hadith" (not in this document); Basran.

### Adam ibn Abi Iyas
- al-Bukhari's teacher in the second chain; biography "introduced in the discussion of the tenth hadith" (not in this document). Given here: from 'Asqalan, grew up in Baghdad; not used by Muslim or Ibn Majah.

### Shu'bah ibn al-Hajjaj
- Biography "presented in the discussion of the tenth hadith" (not in this document). Given here: Basran; restricted his narrations from Qatadah to what Qatadah heard directly.

### Qatadah
- Full name and nasab: Qatadah ibn Di'amah al-Sadusi
- Kunya: Abu al-Khattab
- Nisba(s): al-Sadusi (tribal); al-Basri
- City / region: Basra; said to have been born blind
- Tabaqa: a leading figure of the fourth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy and exceptionally precise" [ثقة ثبت, added]
- Critics' statements: al-Khazraji — distinguished leading scholar, hadith master who practised tadlis — al-Khulasah; al-Dhahabi — trustworthy, exceptionally precise hadith master; practised tadlis; accused of Qadari views — al-Mizan; Yahya ibn Ma'in — the compilers of the authentic collections relied on him, particularly when he said "He narrated to us"; "he died in middle age"; Ibn Hajar — eminent Successor, proverbial memory, occasional tadlis; Ibn Ma'in and others: accused of Qadar; Abu Dawud: "Qatadah's adherence to Qadari beliefs has not been established to our satisfaction" — introduction to Fath al-Bari; Ibn Sirin — "Qatadah has the strongest memory of anyone" — Tahdhib; Bukayr ibn 'Abd Allah al-Muzani — "Of everyone I have seen, he has the strongest memory and is the most likely to transmit a hadith exactly as he heard it" — Tahdhib; Ibn Hajar's Tahdhib entry exceeds five pages and records both his irsal and tadlis
- Teachers mentioned: Anas, Ibn al-Musayyab, Ibn Sirin, many others (al-Khulasah)
- Students mentioned: Ayyub, Humayd, Husayn al-Mu'allim, al-Awza'i, Shu'bah, 'Alqamah (al-Khulasah)
- Born / died: died between 113 and 119 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: tadlis and irsal; accused of Qadar (disputed); "Qatadah" unqualified in al-Bukhari is unambiguous — the only other Qatadah is Qatadah ibn al-Nu'man the Companion.

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- The seven narrators of the two chains are used by all six compilers except Adam ibn Abi Iyas, not used by Muslim or Ibn Majah. [computable: rumuz]
- All seven are Basran except al-Bukhari's two shaykhs: Ya'qub from Baghdad, Adam from 'Asqalan (raised in Baghdad). [computable: region]
- Ya'qub al-Dawraqi was a direct teacher of all six compilers and died four years before al-Bukhari. Ya'qub, Muhammad ibn Bashshar (Bundar) and Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna (al-Zaman) all died in 252 AH and were each teachers of all six; Bundar and al-Zaman were born 167 AH (the year Hammad ibn Salamah died), Ya'qub 166; Ibn Hajar (Taqrib) on al-Zaman and Bundar: "like two evenly matched racehorses, and they died in the same year"; Ziyad ibn Ayyub al-Tusi al-Baghdadi, another teacher of al-Bukhari, also died 252 and was born the same year as Ya'qub. [computable: birth/death years + shaykh-of-six flag]
- All narrators of the first chain are clients (mawali) of Arab tribes except the Companion Anas; Ibn 'Ulayyah's clientage to Banu Asad came through both parents. [computable: mawla field]
- Attribution to the mother: Ibn 'Ulayyah = Isma'il ibn Ibrahim ibn Miqsam. Ibn Hajar (commentary on Nukhbat al-Fikar): knowing those attributed to other than their father matters — al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad (adoptive; paternal line al-Miqdad ibn 'Amr) and those attributed to mothers like Ibn 'Ulayyah. Two rules when father and mother attributions are combined: the second "ibn" takes the same case as the first, and is written with its alif (ابن). Al-Nawawi's list of comparable names: 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn Umm Maktum, 'Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, 'Abd Allah ibn Malik ibn Buhaynah, Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn al-Hanafiyyah, Isma'il ibn Ibrahim ibn 'Ulayyah, Ishaq ibn Ibrahim ibn Rahawayh, Muhammad ibn Yazid ibn Majah (Rahawayh and Majah being sobriquets of the fathers themselves); both attributions are combined so that everyone can identify the person. [computable: name-form field "attributed to mother/other"; scholar: the grammar]
- "Qatadah" without his father's name creates no ambiguity: the only other Qatadah among al-Bukhari's narrators is the Companion Qatadah ibn al-Nu'man. [computable: homonym + generation]
- Highest level of ta'dil via superlatives (Ibn Hajar, commentary on Nukhbat al-Fikar: "the most trustworthy of people", "the most precise", "the ultimate authority in verification"): applied here to Ibn 'Ulayyah (Ahmad; Ibn al-Madini) and Qatadah (Ibn Sirin; Bukayr al-Muzani). [computable if verdict rank is stored; scholar]
- Qatadah's tadlis is neutralised: Shu'bah restricted his narrations from Qatadah to what Qatadah heard, and Qatadah's explicit hearing of this hadith is recorded in al-Nasa'i (Ibn Hajar, Fath). [computable: tadlis flag + student rule + takhrij sama']
- The quoted lafz is Qatadah's because it matches Abu Hurayrah's hadith with the added "and all people" (Fath). [scholar]
- 'Abd al-'Aziz's wording differs ("his family and his wealth" in Ibn Khuzaymah via Ya'qub, in Muslim via Ibn 'Ulayyah, in al-Isma'ili via 'Abd al-Warith; opening "A man does not believe"); al-Bukhari's joining method concerns the underlying hadith, not identical wording (Fath). [scholar]
- The first chain is completed to Anas before ح rather than switched at Anas, perhaps because the formulas from Anas differ: "from the Prophet ﷺ" vs "The Prophet ﷺ said". [scholar]
- The two chains use matching formulas down to Anas: haddathana, haddathana, 'an, 'an. [computable: formula]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary / grammar notes:
  - "none of you believes" — complete faith (Ibn Hajar; al-Munawi, Fayd al-Qadir); obligatory completeness whose neglect is blameworthy, not merely recommended completeness ('Abd al-Rahman ibn Hasan, Fath al-Majid, citing Shaykh al-Islam; Sulayman ibn 'Abd Allah, Taysir al-'Aziz al-Hamid: falling short is a major sin unless it reaches disbelief)
  - walid — may include the mother as "parent", or one of a pair mentioned for both (Fath)
  - "and all people" — general after specific, intended restrictedly; apparently includes one's own self (Fath), confirmed by 'Umar's hadith ("than your own self... Now, O 'Umar", al-Bukhari) and the "sweetness of faith" hadith
  - order "father then child" — precedence in time, respect, universality of having a father; al-Nasa'i's "child then father" reflects tenderness
  - "father and child" vs "family and wealth" — the former more emphatic (Fath)
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari (meaning; variants; walid; scope; order; specific to our Prophet though love of all messengers is part of faith; signs of this love — the test of choosing to see him; supporting his Sunnah, enjoining good); al-Nawawi (the two souls); al-Munawi, Fayd al-Qadir; 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Hasan, Fath al-Majid; Sulayman ibn 'Abd Allah, Taysir al-'Aziz al-Hamid; al-Kirmani (love = resolve to obey); Ibn al-Qayyim, Nuniyyah (verses on precedence of his command and love)
- Qur'an cited: al-Nur 24:47; Al 'Imran 3:31
- Reason for the greater love (as stated): he is the means of guidance out of disbelief; benefit produces love; he receives the reward of all who believe through him
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Obligation to give precedence to love of the Messenger ﷺ over those dearest.
  2. Loving the Messenger ﷺ is obligatory and inseparable from love of Allah.
  3. Deeds are part of faith (love is an action of the heart).
  4. Faith may be negated from a person while he remains within Islam.
  5. If this is the status of love for the Messenger, what of love for Allah?
  6. Respect due to a parent and tenderness due to a child.
  7. The most important matter is mentioned first.
  8. The importance of reflection, through which the greater love becomes apparent (Ibn Hajar's extended passage in Fath al-Bari).
