## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: أَنَّ رَجُلاً سَأَلَ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: أَيُّ الإِسْلاَمِ خَيْرٌ؟ قَالَ: «تُطْعِمُ الطَّعَامَ، وَتَقْرَأُ السَّلاَمَ عَلَى مَنْ عَرَفْتَ وَمَنْ لَمْ تَعْرِفْ»
- English gist: Asked which part of Islam is best, the Prophet ﷺ said: feeding others and greeting with salam both those you know and those you do not.
- Kitab / bab: Book of Faith, chapter "Providing Food Is Part of Islam".

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. al-Bukhari | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → 'Amr ibn Khalid | 
2. 'Amr ibn Khalid | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Al-Layth | = al-Layth ibn Sa'd
3. Al-Layth | "from" (عن) → Yazid | = Yazid ibn Abi Habib; in the Isti'dhan route al-Layth says "Yazid narrated to me" (حدثني)
4. Yazid ibn Abi Habib | "from" (عن) → Abu al-Khayr | = Marthad ibn 'Abdullah al-Yazani; Tabi'i from Tabi'i
5. Abu al-Khayr | "from" (عن) → 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr | 
6. 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr | "that a man asked the Prophet ﷺ" (أن رجلاً سأل النبي ﷺ) | the questioner is unnamed

Five narrators between al-Bukhari and the Prophet ﷺ, all Egyptian.

## Narrators

### 'Amr ibn Khalid
- Full name and nasab: 'Amr ibn Khalid ibn Farrukh ibn Sa'id al-Tamimi, also said al-Khuza'i
- Kunya: Abu al-Hasan
- Nisba(s): al-Tamimi / al-Khuza'i (tribal, disputed); al-Harrani; settled in Egypt
- City / region: Harran origin; Egypt
- Tabaqa: tenth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: al-'Ijli, al-Daraqutni, Maslamah — declared him trustworthy — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib; Ibn Hibban — in al-Thiqat — Tahdhib; al-Dhahabi — "A well-known, trustworthy narrator" — al-Mizan
- Teachers mentioned: al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Zuhayr ibn Mu'awiyah (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari (several places)
- Born / died: died 229 AH, in Egypt (Taqrib; al-Maqdisi)
- Rumuz: al-Bukhari and Ibn Majah

### Al-Layth ibn Sa'd
- Full name and nasab: al-Layth ibn Sa'd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Fahmi
- Kunya: Abu al-Harith
- Nisba(s): al-Fahmi (by clientage); al-Misri
- Mawla status: "their client" (mawla of Fahm) — al-Khulasah
- City / region: Egypt; mufti of the people of Egypt in his time
- Tabaqa: seventh generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy, thoroughly reliable, a jurist and a renowned imam" [ثقة ثبت فقيه إمام مشهور, added]
- Critics' statements: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Ma'in, Ibn al-Madini, al-'Ijli, al-Nasa'i, Ya'qub ibn Shaybah — trustworthy — Tahdhib; Ibn Hibban — "among the leading people of his time in jurisprudence, piety, knowledge, virtue and generosity" — al-Thiqat; Ibn Abi Maryam — "The finest of Allah's creation whom I ever saw was al-Layth"; Abu Ya'la al-Khalili — "the undisputed imam of his time"; al-Dhahabi — eminent, firmly established, trustworthy, authority by unanimous agreement — al-Mizan; al-Khazraji — imam, scholar, jurist and leader of Egypt — al-Khulasah; al-Nawawi (Sharh Muslim) — al-Shafi'i and Ibn Bukayr testified he had greater understanding of fiqh than Malik; Muhammad ibn Rumh — annual income 80,000 dinars yet zakat never became due; Qutaybah — Malik sent him Madinan produce, al-Layth sent back 1,000 dinars
- Born / died: died Sha'ban 175 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: Ibn Hajar wrote a monograph on him, al-Rahmah al-Ghaythiyyah bi al-Tarjamah al-Laythiyyah, in eight chapters "corresponding to the gates of Paradise".

### Yazid ibn Abi Habib
- Full name and nasab: Yazid ibn Abi Habib al-Misri; his father's name Suwayd
- Kunya: Abu Raja'
- Nisba(s): al-Misri
- Mawla status: "differing reports concerning his clientage"
- City / region: Egypt; mufti of the people of Egypt in his time (Ibn Yunus)
- Tabaqa: fifth generation (Taqrib); a Tabi'i
- Taqrib grading line: "a trustworthy jurist who sometimes narrated in the disconnected form" [ثقة فقيه وكان يرسل, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Sa'd, Abu Zur'ah, al-'Ijli — trustworthy — Tahdhib; Ibn Hibban — al-Thiqat; Ibn Abi Hatim from his father — "his narration from 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir is disconnected"; Ibn Yunus (via al-Nawawi) — mufti of Egypt, forbearing and intelligent, first to spread knowledge of halal and haram in Egypt (before him people discussed tribulations, end-times battles and exhortation)
- Born / died: died 128 AH, approaching eighty (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: irsal (mursal narration), specifically from 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir.

### Abu al-Khayr
- Full name and nasab: Marthad ibn 'Abdullah al-Yazani (initial ya', then zay, then nun)
- Kunya: Abu al-Khayr
- Nisba(s): al-Yazani — Yazan is a branch of Himyar (al-Maqdisi); al-Misri
- City / region: Egypt; mufti of the people of Egypt in his time (Ibn Yunus)
- Tabaqa: third generation (Taqrib); a Tabi'i
- Taqrib grading line: "a trustworthy jurist" [ثقة فقيه, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Yunus — mufti of Egypt; al-'Ijli — "a trustworthy Tabi'i"; Ibn Sa'd — trustworthy, virtue and devotion in worship; Ya'qub ibn Sufyan — trustworthy — all Tahdhib
- Teachers mentioned (al-Maqdisi): 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir, 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr, Abu 'Abdullah al-Sunabihi, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Usaylah (both Sahihs); 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Wa'lah (Muslim)
- Students mentioned (al-Maqdisi): Yazid ibn Abi Habib (both Sahihs); Ja'far ibn Rabi'ah, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Shumasah (Muslim)
- Born / died: died 90 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six

### 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr (Companion)
- Full name and nasab: 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As ibn Wa'il ibn Hashim ibn Su'ayd (diminutive) ibn Sa'd ibn Sahm al-Sahmi
- Kunya: Abu Muhammad; also said Abu 'Abd al-Rahman
- Nisba(s): al-Sahmi
- City / region: Makkah, then Syria, then Egypt (al-Maqdisi)
- Companion facts: among the early Muslims; accepted Islam before his father; narrated many hadiths; one of the four learned jurists called the 'Abadilah; diligent in worship and abundant in knowledge (Tahdhib); the age difference between him and his father was eleven years (al-Khulasah, Tahdhib) or thirteen (al-Maqdisi)
- Teachers mentioned: the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (both Sahihs); 'Umar, Abu al-Darda', Mu'adh, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, his father 'Amr (al-Isabah)
- Students mentioned: Masruq, Abu al-Khayr Marthad, Humayd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, Abu al-'Abbas al-Sha'ir (al-Maqdisi); Companions who narrated from him (Abu Nu'aym): Ibn 'Umar, Abu Umamah, al-Miswar, al-Sa'ib ibn Yazid, Abu al-Tufayl
- Hadith counts: 700 total (al-Khazraji, al-Khulasah); agreed 17; al-Bukhari alone 8; Muslim alone 20; Ibn Hajar (introduction to Fath): 26 in al-Bukhari
- Died: Dhu al-Hijjah around the incident of al-Harrah, in Ta'if (stronger views, Taqrib); Yahya ibn Bukayr: 65 AH; another view 68 AH aged 92
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: al-Nawawi (Sharh Muslim) on his eminence, fiqh, abundant hadith, piety, prayer and fasting.

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- All five narrators are used by the six compilers except al-Bukhari's shaykh 'Amr ibn Khalid, used only by al-Bukhari and Ibn Majah. [computable: rumuz]
- All five narrators are Egyptian — an all-Egyptian chain, which is uncommon; al-Nawawi (Sharh Muslim) counts it among the rare chains in Muslim and elsewhere, rarer still given the narrators' eminence. [computable: region]
- Four of the six compilers — al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i — narrate it with the same chain and wording, all from Qutaybah ibn Sa'id. [computable: takhrij chains]
- The questioner is unnamed (Ibn Hajar, Fath) — mubham in the matn. [scholar]
- Three successive muftis of Egypt in one chain: Abu al-Khayr, Yazid ibn Abi Habib, al-Layth ibn Sa'd. [scholar; computable only with a "role" field]
- A Tabi'i narrating from a Tabi'i: Yazid ibn Abi Habib from Abu al-Khayr. [computable: generation]
- 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr is one of the four 'Abadilah: 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar, ibn 'Amr, ibn 'Abbas, ibn al-Zubayr (previously discussed in the teacher's second hadith). [scholar]
- The age difference between 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr and his father 'Amr ibn al-'As is reported as eleven or thirteen years. [computable from birth dates; scholar]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary / grammar notes:
  - khayr — here an elative ("best"), original form akhyar with the hamzah dropped; elsewhere a noun "goodness"; both uses in al-Anfal 8:70 (يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ قُلْ لِمَنْ فِي أَيْدِيكُمْ مِنَ الْأَسْرَىٰ إِنْ يَعْلَمِ اللَّهُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ خَيْرًا يُؤْتِكُمْ خَيْرًا مِمَّا أُخِذَ مِنْكُمْ)
  - the question follows an omitted verb "saying", explicit in Ibn Majah's wording
  - tut'im al-ta'am — understood as a verbal noun ("that you provide food"), like "Your hearing about al-Mu'aydi..." (Fath); the particle "an" is explicit in Ahmad's Musnad
  - "providing food" — general, including hospitality and other feeding (Fath)
  - taqra' al-salam — the verb "to read" with the sense "to say"; Abu Hatim al-Sijistani: "iqra' 'alayhi al-salam" (convey) vs "aqri'hu al-salam" (written greeting), though the latter also occurs orally (Ibrahim's words "Convey my salam to your Ummah")
  - "those whom you know and those whom you do not know" — greet all without selectivity out of pride; generality qualified by other evidence (disbeliever, hypocrite, open sinner); or the prohibition came later (Fath); al-Nawawi: specific to Muslims, no initial salam to a disbeliever; one whose religion is unknown may be greeted as a precaution; Ibn Battal: opens communication and brotherhood, selective greeting resembles prohibited shunning
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari (grammar; meaning of feeding; taqra'; scope of the greeting); al-Nawawi, Sharh Muslim (scope; narrators' virtues; rarity of the Egyptian chain); Abu Hatim al-Sijistani; Ibn Battal
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. The Companions' eagerness to pursue goodness and learn the truth.
  2. Religious matters are referred to scholars.
  3. The qualities of Islam differ in degrees of virtue.
  4. Seek the most virtuous qualities and attend to them.
  5. Deeds sincerely for Allah, hoping for His reward.
  6. Encouragement of humility and guiding others to it.
  7. Spreading salam is among the best qualities of Islam.
  8. Providing food is among the best qualities of Islam.
  9. The virtue of honouring guests.
  10. Salam to those one knows and those one does not.
  11. One not greeted may turn out to be an acquaintance and feel alienated.
  12. Means of bringing hearts together: gifts, food, salam and the like.
