# Sahih al-Bukhari 3688

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 6" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Hadith Six", ID `13gNlfMwDgGVK1gMbP-wr0Wlp4FhptyHo8XQBEGzHp4Y`). The notes give no Fath al-Bari number. [added] Identified as al-Bukhari **3688** (Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering; Faḍāʾil aṣ-Ṣaḥābah, bāb manāqib ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb) from the chain and chapter stated in the notes.

## Text
- Arabic matn: not given in the notes (English only). [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:3688] أَنَّ رَجُلًا سَأَلَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ فَقَالَ: مَتَى السَّاعَةُ؟ قَالَ: «وَمَاذَا أَعْدَدْتَ لَهَا؟» قَالَ: لَا شَيْءَ، إِلَّا أَنِّي أُحِبُّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ ﷺ. فَقَالَ: «أَنْتَ مَعَ مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ». قَالَ أَنَسٌ: فَمَا فَرِحْنَا بِشَيْءٍ فَرَحَنَا بِقَوْلِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ: «أَنْتَ مَعَ مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ». قَالَ أَنَسٌ: فَأَنَا أُحِبُّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ وَأَبَا بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ، وَأَرْجُو أَنْ أَكُونَ مَعَهُمْ بِحُبِّي إِيَّاهُمْ، وَإِنْ لَمْ أَعْمَلْ بِمِثْلِ أَعْمَالِهِمْ
- English as rendered: a man asked the Prophet ﷺ "When will the Hour be?"; "What have you prepared for it?"; "Nothing, except that I love Allah and His Messenger"; "You will be with those whom you love." Anas: "We never rejoiced over anything as much as we rejoiced at [this]"; "I love the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and Umar, and I hope I will be with them because of my love for them, even though I do not perform deeds equal to theirs."
- Gist: a man asks when the Hour will come; the Prophet ﷺ redirects him to what he has prepared, and on hearing that he loves Allah and His Messenger tells him he will be with those he loves; Anas adds the Companions' joy and his own hope.
- Kitab / bab: Book of the Virtues of the Companions, "the chapter concerning the virtues of Umar ibn al-Khattab"

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. Sulayman ibn Harb | ḥaddathanā ("narrated to us") | Basran; al-Bukhārī's shaykh
2. Hammad ibn Zayd | ḥaddathanā | Basran
3. Thabit [al-Bunani] | ʿan | Basran; Tābiʿī, c. 40 years with Anas
4. Anas [ibn Malik] | ʿan | Companion; settled in Basra; then the man's exchange with the Prophet ﷺ

Four links between al-Bukhārī and the Prophet ﷺ — one of his rubāʿiyyāt.

## Narrators

### Sulayman ibn Harb
- Full name and nasab: Sulayman ibn Harb ibn Bujayl al-Azdi al-Washahi
- Kunya: Abu Ayyub
- Laqab / title: imām, ḥāfiẕ
- Nisba(s): al-Azdi al-Washahi
- Mawla status: not stated
- City / region: Basra (lata'if)
- Tabaqa: ninth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Trustworthy, an Imam, a memoriser (hafiz), from the ninth generation"
- Critics' statements: Abu Hatim, Yahya ibn Aktham, Yaʿqub ibn Shaybah, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Khirash, Ibn Hibban, Ibn Qaniʿ, Ibn ʿAdi, Ibn Saʿd — trustworthy — (book not named); "the scholars unanimously praised and authenticated him"
- Teachers mentioned: Shuʿbah, "the two Hammads", Jarir ibn Hazim
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud al-Sijistani, Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ishaq ibn Rahawayh, "Yahya al-Qattan" (so in notes), Muhammad ibn Jaʿfar
- Died: 224 AH, aged approximately eighty
- Rumuz: all six

### Hammad ibn Zayd
- Full name and nasab: Hammad ibn Zayd ibn Dirham al-Azdi al-Jahdami
- Kunya: Abu Ismaʿil
- Laqab / title: one of "the two Hammads" (with Hammad ibn Salamah)
- Nisba(s): al-Azdi al-Jahdami; al-Basri
- Mawla status: not stated
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: not stated
- Grading (Ibn Ḥajar, as rendered): "Trustworthy, firmly established, a jurist"
- Critics' statements:
  - ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi — the leaders of their time were four: Sufyan al-Thawri in Kufah, Malik in the Hijaz, al-Awzaʿi in Sham, Hammad ibn Zayd in Basra — (book not named)
  - Wakiʿ ibn al-Jarrah — of the two Hammads, Hammad ibn Zayd had the stronger memory; "we used to compare him only to Misʿar" — (book not named)
  - Yahya ibn Yahya al-Naysaburi — "I have never seen anyone with a stronger memory than him" — (book not named)
- Teachers mentioned: Thabit al-Bunani, Anas ibn Sirin, ʿAbd al-ʿAziz ibn Suhayb, ʿAsim al-Ahwal
- Students mentioned: Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Mahdi, Ibn Wahb, Yahya al-Qattan, Sufyan ibn ʿUyaynah, Sufyan al-Thawri, ʿArim, Musaddad, Sulayman ibn Harb
- Died: 179 AH, "the same year that Malik ibn Anas died"; approximate age 81 (lata'if table)
- Rumuz: all six (lata'if point 2)
- Notable: became blind later in life, though authentically reported to have written earlier

### Thabit al-Bunani
- Full name and nasab: Thabit ibn Aslam al-Bunani
- Kunya: Abu Muhammad
- Nisba(s): al-Bunani (Bunanah, descendants of Banu Saʿd ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib — Abu al-Fadl Ibn Tahir); al-Basri
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: fourth
- Grading (Ibn Ḥajar, as rendered): "Trustworthy and devoted in worship"
- Critics' statements: al-ʿIjli, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Saʿd — trustworthy — (book not named)
- Teachers mentioned: Ibn ʿUmar, ʿAbdullah ibn Mughaffal, Anas ibn Malik, other Companions
- Students mentioned: Shuʿbah, Hammad ibn Zayd, Hammad ibn Salamah, Maʿmar
- Died: "around 123–127 AH", aged 86

### Anas ibn Malik
- Full name and nasab: Anas ibn Malik ibn al-Nadr al-Ansari al-Khazraji
- Kunya: Abu Hamzah
- Laqab / title: servant of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ for ten years; "a well-known Companion" (Ibn Ḥajar)
- Nisba(s): al-Ansari al-Khazraji
- City / region: settled in Basra; the last Companion to die in Basra
- Narrated from: the Prophet ﷺ; Abu Bakr, Muʿadh ibn Jabal, Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, others
- Students mentioned: al-Zuhri, Yahya ibn Saʿid, al-Hasan al-Basri, Qatadah, Thabit al-Bunani, Humayd
- Hadith counts: 1,286 total; 168 agreed; 83 al-Bukhari only; 71 Muslim only (al-Khazraji). Number in al-Bukhari: 268 (Ibn Ḥajar)
- Died: 92 AH, or 93 AH, aged over one hundred
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: Ibn Ḥajar, al-Isabah: "The virtues and merits of Anas are extremely numerous"

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All four narrators are Basran — a chain musalsal with Basrans; and one of al-Bukhārī's rubāʿiyyāt (four links to the Prophet ﷺ). [computable]
2. All four are narrated from by all six compilers (al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah). [computable]
3. Ages descend step by step along the chain: Anas over 100, Thabit 86, Hammad 81, Sulayman 80, al-Bukhari 62. [computable]
4. First letters of the names ascend in Arabic alphabetical order: أ (Anas), ث (Thabit), ح (Hammad), س (Sulayman), م (Muhammad) — so their rijāl entries fall in chain order. [computable]
5. "The two Hammads" (al-Khulasah on Thabit and on Sulayman) = Hammad ibn Zayd and Hammad ibn Salamah, same generation, Basrans; al-Bukhari narrates extensively from Hammad ibn Zayd but cites Hammad ibn Salamah only in taʿlīqāt. [computable from rumuz data; the taʿlīq point is scholar]
6. "Thabit" is Thabit ibn Aslam al-Bunani, famous for narrating from Anas because of c. forty years' companionship. [computable with teacher-student data]
7. "Anas" is unambiguous even bare, since no other Companion named Anas appears in al-Bukhari's chains; some versions give his full name. [computable]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - "a man" — in some of Anas's narrations "a man from the Bedouins"; Ibn Ḥajar (Fatḥ al-Bārī) identifies him as Dhu al-Khuwaysirah al-Yamani, the Bedouin who urinated in the mosque, citing ad-Dāraquṭnī from Ibn Masʿūd
  - "What have you prepared for it?" — what righteous deeds whose reward you hope to meet; al-Kirmani (via Ibn Ḥajar): uslūb al-ḥakīm, redirecting the questioner to what is more beneficial
  - "You are with those whom you love" — joined to them and counted in their company (Ibn Ḥajar); companionship in Paradise does not require equality of rank
- Commentaries cited: Fatḥ al-Bārī (identity of the questioner; al-Kirmani on uslūb al-ḥakīm; meaning of "with"; Abū Nuʿaym's monograph); Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr (tawātur). Āl ʿImrān 3:31 cited on love requiring following; love of Abū Bakr and ʿUmar is part of love of the Prophet ﷺ; the Ahl as-Sunnah order of virtue: Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī; Muslim's wording of Anas's joy.
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Referring to scholars and asking them about religion.
  2. Gentleness in teaching and directing the questioner to what benefits most.
  3. The Prophet's ﷺ perfect sincerity and concern for his Ummah.
  4. Occupying oneself with what matters and leaving what does not.
  5. Preparing for the Hereafter rather than matters of little benefit.
  6. Not being deluded by one's deeds (the man's humility).
  7. The great status of loving Allah and His Messenger (hadith of the three qualities and the sweetness of faith, al-Bukhārī and Muslim).
  8. The questioner's insight and sincerity in his answer.
  9. Loving the truth and its people, since "a person is with those whom he loves".
  10. The Companions' excellence shown by their joy at the statement.
  11. A ruling addressed to one applies to all unless evidence restricts it.
  12. The virtue of Abū Bakr and ʿUmar — a reason al-Bukhārī placed it under the virtues of ʿUmar.
  13. The Companions' love for Abū Bakr and ʿUmar.
  14. Permissibility of seeking nearness to Allah through one's righteous deeds (Anas's hope; the hadith of the three men in the cave).
