# Sahih al-Bukhari 57

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 9" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Hadith Nine", ID `1bbo0XdtvpQfOOBqMPogghHKOaZ5Z97eAF6Rsl7qRTBI`). The notes give no Fath al-Bari number. [added] Identified as al-Bukhari **57** (Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering; end of Kitab al-Iman, bab qawl an-Nabi ﷺ "ad-din an-nasihah...") from the chain and chapter stated in the notes.

## Text
- Arabic matn: not given in the notes (English only). [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:57] بَايَعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ عَلَى إِقَامِ الصَّلاَةِ، وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ، وَالنُّصْحِ لِكُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ
- English as rendered: "I pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to establish the prayer, pay zakah, and offer sincere counsel to every Muslim."
- Gist: Jarīr reports the terms on which he gave the Prophet ﷺ his pledge — prayer, zakāh and sincere counsel (naṣīḥah) to every Muslim.
- Kitab / bab: "towards the end of the Book of Faith", chapter "The Statement of the Prophet ﷺ: 'Religion is sincere counsel to Allah, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims, and the Muslim community in general'"

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. Musaddad | ḥaddathanā, qāla | Basran; al-Bukhārī's shaykh; not used by Muslim or Ibn Mājah
2. Yahya [ibn Saʿid al-Qattan] | ḥaddathanā ("narrated to us") | Basran
3. Ismaʿil [ibn Abi Khalid] | ʿan | Kufan; Bajalī (mawlā); kunya Abu ʿAbdullah
4. Qays ibn Abi Hazim | qāla ḥaddathanī ("narrated to me") | Kufan; Bajalī; mukhaḍram; kunya Abu ʿAbdullah
5. Jarir ibn ʿAbdullah | ʿan ... qāla | Companion; Bajalī; settled in Kufah; kunya Abu ʿAmr or Abu ʿAbdullah

## Narrators

### Musaddad
- Full name and nasab: Musaddad ibn Musarhad ibn Musarbal ibn Mustawrid al-Asadi al-Basri (Taqrīb); the lata'if extend it "...ibn Mustawrid ibn Marʿabal"; "it is also said that his name was ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAziz and that Musaddad was his title (laqab)"
- Kunya: Abu al-Hasan
- Laqab / title: Musaddad (possibly a laqab); ḥāfiẕ; "said to be the first to compile a Musnad in Basrah"
- Nisba(s): al-Asadi; al-Basri
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: tenth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy and a hadith master... belonged to the tenth generation and died in 228 AH"
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib unless noted):
  - Ibn Maʿin (via Jaʿfar ibn Abi ʿUthman) — "Record from Musaddad, for he is trustworthy, trustworthy"
  - Ibn Maʿin (via Muhammad ibn Harun al-Fallas) — truthful (ṣadūq)
  - al-Nasa'i — trustworthy
  - al-ʿIjli — trustworthy; anecdote of his dictating fifty hadiths after al-ʿIjli was weary; Abu Nuʿaym on his lineage: "Ahmad, this is an incantation for a scorpion sting"
  - Abu Hatim (via Ibn Abi Hatim) — trustworthy
  - Ibn Qaniʿ — trustworthy
  - Ibn Hibban — in al-Thiqat
- Teachers mentioned: ʿAbdullah ibn Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, Hushaym, Wakiʿ, al-Qattan, Ibn ʿUlayyah, others
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud, others
- Died: 228 AH
- Rumuz: the major collections "apart from those of Muslim and Ibn Majah"

### Yahya al-Qattan
- Full name and nasab: Yahya ibn Saʿid ibn Farrukh al-Tamimi
- Kunya: Abu Saʿid
- Laqab / title: al-Ahwal; al-Qattan; ḥāfiẕ, ḥujjah, "one of the leading scholars of narrator criticism and accreditation" (al-Khulasah); "imām, qudwah" (Taqrīb)
- Nisba(s): al-Tamimi; al-Basri
- Mawla status: a client of Banu Tamim (al-Maqdisi)
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: "senior scholars of the ninth generation" (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy, precise, a hadith master, a leading scholar, and an exemplary authority"; died 198 AH aged 78
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib, two pages of praise; among them):
  - ʿAli ibn al-Madini — never saw anyone more reliable and precise
  - Ibrahim ibn Muhammad al-Taymi — never saw anyone more knowledgeable about narrators
  - Ahmad (via ʿAbdullah ibn Ahmad) — "my eyes have never seen anyone like him"; "the highest standard of careful verification in Basrah"
  - Bundar (via Ibn Khuzaymah) — "the leading scholar of the people of his time"
  - Ibn Saʿd — trustworthy, reliable, distinguished, an authority
  - al-ʿIjli — trustworthy Basran who narrated only from trustworthy people
  - Abu Zurʿah — among the trustworthy hadith masters
  - Abu Hatim — an authoritative hadith master
  - al-Nasa'i — trustworthy, firmly reliable, approved
  - al-Thawri marvelled at his memory; the scholars: "Anyone whom Yahya abandoned, we also abandoned"
- Teachers mentioned: Sulayman al-Taymi, Humayd al-Tawil, Ismaʿil ibn Abi Khalid, many others
- Students mentioned: his son Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Saʿid, his grandson Ahmad ibn Muhammad, Ahmad, Ishaq, ʿAli ibn al-Madini, Yahya ibn Maʿin, ʿAmr ibn ʿAli al-Fallas, Musaddad
- Died: 198 AH, aged 78
- Rumuz: all the major collections

### Ismaʿil ibn Abi Khalid
- Full name and nasab: Ismaʿil ibn Abi Khalid al-Ahmasi al-Bajali; Abu Khalid's name was Saʿd, or Hurmuz, or Kathir (al-Maqdisi)
- Kunya: Abu ʿAbdullah
- Nisba(s): al-Ahmasi al-Bajali; al-Kufi (al-Maqdisi, and lata'if). The notes' quotation of the Taqrīb reads "al-Ahmasi, their client, al-Basri" — (unclear in notes; the rest of the document treats him as Kufan)
- Mawla status: "their client" (of Ahmas / Bajilah)
- City / region: Kufah
- Tabaqa: fourth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy and firmly reliable. He belonged to the fourth generation and died in 146 AH"
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib):
  - al-Thawri (via Ibn al-Mubarak) — "The hadith masters among the people were three: Ismaʿil, ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman, and Yahya ibn Saʿid al-Ansari"
  - "the most knowledgeable person regarding al-Shaʿbi and the most reliable narrator of his reports"
  - Ibn Mahdi, Ibn Maʿin, al-Nasa'i, al-ʿIjli, Yaʿqub ibn Abi Shaybah, Abu Hatim, Yaʿqub ibn Sulayman — trustworthy
- Teachers mentioned: a group of Companions and Successors; "narrated extensively from Qays ibn Abi Hazim"
- Students mentioned: Shuʿbah, the two Sufyans, Za'idah, Ibn al-Mubarak, Hushaym, Yahya al-Qattan, Yazid ibn Harun, ʿUbaydullah ibn Musa (the last trustworthy narrator from him), Yahya ibn Hashim al-Simsar (abandoned; the last person ever to narrate from him); al-Hakam ibn ʿUtaybah (lata'if)
- Died: 146 AH
- Rumuz: all the major collections
- Notable: sābiq wa lāḥiq example — al-Hakam ibn ʿUtaybah and Yahya ibn Hashim both narrated from him, their deaths about 110 years apart (al-Khatib, via Tahdhib)

### Qays ibn Abi Hazim
- Full name and nasab: Qays ibn Abi Hazim al-Bajali al-Ahmasi; his father's name Husayn ibn ʿAwf, or ʿAwf ibn ʿAbd al-Harith, or ʿAbd ʿAwf ibn al-Harith ibn ʿAwf (Tahdhib)
- Kunya: Abu ʿAbdullah
- Nisba(s): al-Bajali al-Ahmasi; al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufah
- Tabaqa: second (Taqrīb); mukhaḍram
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy... a mukhadram, having lived during both the pre-Islamic period and Islam. It is also said that he saw the Prophet ﷺ. He is said to have had the distinction of narrating from all ten Companions who were promised Paradise. He died after the year 90 AH, or shortly before it, after having lived beyond the age of one hundred. His memory changed towards the end of his life"
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Khirash — a distinguished Kufan; alone among the Successors narrated from all ten promised Paradise — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib
  - al-Dhahabi — trustworthy and an authority, "very nearly attained the status of a Companion"; Ibn Maʿin and others declared him trustworthy; "the scholars unanimously accepted his narrations; whoever criticised him only harmed himself" — al-Mizan
- Teachers mentioned: a large group of Companions (named in Tahdhib; not listed in notes)
- Students mentioned: Ismaʿil ibn Abi Khalid, others
- Died: after 90 AH or shortly before, aged over 100
- Rumuz: all the major collections
- Notable: travelled to pledge allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ, who died while Qays was on the way; his father was a Companion; whether he saw the Prophet ﷺ is not established; memory changed late in life (ikhtilāṭ)

### Jarir ibn ʿAbdullah
- Full name and nasab: Jarir ibn ʿAbdullah ibn Jabir — whose name was al-Salil — ibn Malik ibn Nadr ibn Thaʿlabah ibn Jusham ibn ʿAwf al-Bajali al-Qasri (Tahdhib)
- Kunya: Abu ʿAmr; also given Abu ʿAbdullah al-Yamani
- Laqab / title: "the Yusuf of this Ummah" (ʿUmar), for his beauty; "a well-known Companion"
- Nisba(s): al-Bajali al-Qasri; al-Yamani
- City / region: settled in Kufah; later withdrew to Qarqisiya
- Narrated from: the Prophet ﷺ, ʿUmar, Muʿawiyah
- Students mentioned: his sons al-Mundhir, ʿUbaydullah, Ayyub, Ibrahim; his grandson Abu Zurʿah ibn ʿAmr; Anas; Abu Wa'il; Zayd ibn Wahb; Ziyad ibn ʿIlaqah; al-Shaʿbi; Qays ibn Abi Hazim; others
- Hadith counts (al-Khulasah): 100 total; 8 agreed; 1 al-Bukhari only; 6 Muslim only. Number in al-Bukhari: 10 (Ibn Ḥajar, introduction to Fath al-Bari)
- Islam / events: leader of his people; ʿUmar appointed him over all Bajilah in the Iraq wars; major role at al-Qadisiyyah; ʿAli sent him as envoy to Muʿawiyah; withdrew from both parties to Qarqisiya (al-Isabah); the Prophet ﷺ sent him to Dhu al-Khalasah, which he demolished
- Died: 51 AH (Taqrīb: "although a later date has also been mentioned"); al-Isabah: 51 AH, "although 54 AH has also been stated"
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿUmayr: "his face was like a portion of the moon"; ʿUmar: "What an excellent leader you were in the pre-Islamic period, and what an excellent leader you are in Islam"

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All five narrators are used by the six compilers except Musaddad, from whom Muslim and Ibn Majah narrated nothing. [computable]
2. Al-Bukhārī's shaykh Musaddad and his shaykh Yahya al-Qattan are both Basran. [computable]
3. Jarir, Qays and Ismaʿil share four features: all of Bajilah; all Kufan; all with kunya Abu ʿAbdullah; all used by the six compilers. [computable]
4. Sābiq wa lāḥiq: al-Hakam ibn ʿUtaybah and Yahya ibn Hashim both narrated from Ismaʿil ibn Abi Khalid, with about 110 years between their deaths (al-Khatib, via Tahdhib); "a similar example was previously mentioned among the subtle features of the chain of the third hadith" ([added] teacher's Hadith 3 = `bukhari-335.md` in this folder). [computable from death dates]
5. Qays ibn Abi Hazim is a mukhaḍram and said to be the only Successor who narrated from all ten promised Paradise (al-Taqrib). [computable flag; the "all ten" claim is scholar data]
6. Jarir is "the Yusuf of this Ummah" (al-Isabah). [scholar]
7. Musaddad's lineage (Musaddad ibn Musarhad ibn Musarbal ibn Mustawrid ibn Marʿabal) — Abu Nuʿaym: "an incantation for a scorpion sting" (Tahdhib; al-Jamʿ bayna Rijal al-Sahihayn). [scholar]
8. Al-Bukhārī rarely repeats a hadith with identical chain and wording (Ibn Tahir al-Maqdisi via the introduction to Fath al-Bari; Ibn Ḥajar on the hadith of ingratitude to husbands in Kitab al-Iman); this hadith is one of the rare cases — identical in Kitab al-Iman and Kitab al-Shurut. [computable from chain + matn comparison]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - النصيحة — al-Mazari (via Fath al-Bari): from "I purified (naṣaḥtu) the honey", i.e. filtered it, so sincere words are pure words; or from naṣaḥa, to sew with a minṣaḥah (needle), repairing a brother's affairs as a needle mends cloth — hence tawbah naṣūḥ, repentance that stitches what sin tore
  - "to every Muslim" — reflects the usual recipients; counsel is also owed to a disbeliever by inviting him to Islam and advising him rightly when he asks
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari — the pledge is the pledge of Islam; after tawḥīd the first condition was prayer (foremost bodily worship), then zakāh (foremost financial worship), then each group's particular need; Jarir, as leader of his people, was made to pledge naṣīḥah so he would teach them; the ʿAbd al-Qays delegation pledged the khums because they fought the disbelievers of Mudar. The teacher adds: recipients of counsel are the Muslim authorities (hearing and obeying in what is right, assisting in good, warning against evil, supplicating for them) and Muslims in general (guiding to good, warning from evil, loving for them what one loves for oneself).
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. The Companions pledged allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ upon Islam.
  2. The importance and status of prayer and zakāh.
  3. Prayer and zakāh are paired here as in the Qurʾān.
  4. Prayer precedes zakāh as the greatest pillar after tawḥīd.
  5. The great importance the Lawgiver gives to sincere counsel.
  6. A Muslim should offer sincere counsel and extend it to everyone.
