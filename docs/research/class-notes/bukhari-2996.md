# Sahih al-Bukhari 2996

> Source: teacher's class notes, Google Doc "Hadith 5" (course sequence number 5; the notes do not give the Bukhari number). Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com number **2996** is [added], confirmed against the Arabic text of 2996 (isnad matches). The notes are in English; Arabic isnad/matn below is [added]; Arabic grading terms in brackets are [added] reconstructions of the Taqrib's standard wording.

## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: إِذَا مَرِضَ الْعَبْدُ أَوْ سَافَرَ، كُتِبَ لَهُ مِثْلُ مَا كَانَ يَعْمَلُ مُقِيمًا صَحِيحًا
- English gist: When a servant falls ill or travels, he is credited with the like of what he used to do while resident and healthy.
- Kitab / bab: Book of Jihād, chapter "A traveller is recorded as having the same deeds he used to perform while resident".

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. al-Bukhārī | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Maṭar ibn al-Faḍl | 
2. Maṭar ibn al-Faḍl | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Yazīd ibn Hārūn | 
3. Yazīd ibn Hārūn | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → al-ʿAwwām | named without nasab (= Ibn Ḥawshab)
4. al-ʿAwwām | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Ibrāhīm Abū Ismāʿīl al-Saksakī | 
5. Ibrāhīm al-Saksakī | "I heard" (سمعت) → Abū Burdah | with the story: Abū Burdah and Yazīd ibn Abī Kabshah travelling together, Yazīd fasting on the journey
6. Abū Burdah | "I heard Abū Mūsā repeatedly saying" (سمعت ... مرارًا) → Abū Mūsā | son from father
7. Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī | "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said" (قال رسول الله ﷺ) | 

Six narrators between al-Bukhārī and the Prophet ﷺ. Yazīd ibn Abī Kabshah is a person in the story, not a narrator.

## Narrators

### Maṭar ibn al-Faḍl
- Full name and nasab: Maṭar ibn al-Faḍl al-Marwazī
- Nisba(s): al-Marwazī
- City / region: Marw; died in Farbar (al-Firabrī: "He died among us")
- Tabaqa: eleventh generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Ḥibbān — "His narrations are sound" — (quoted after Khulāṣat Tadhhīb al-Kamāl; book not named)
- Teachers mentioned: Wakīʿ and others (al-Khulāṣah); Yazīd ibn Hārūn, Shabābah, Rūḥ ibn ʿUbādah (al-Maqdisī)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhārī, in the chapters of Prayer, Jihād and the Hijrah of the Prophet ﷺ (al-Maqdisī)
- Born / died: died after 250 AH (al-Firabrī)
- Rumuz: al-Bukhārī only

### Yazīd ibn Hārūn
- Full name and nasab: Yazīd ibn Hārūn ibn Zādhān al-Sulamī
- Kunya: Abū Khālid
- Nisba(s): al-Sulamī (by clientage); al-Wāsiṭī
- Mawla status: "their freed slave" (mawla of Banū Sulaym)
- City / region: Wāsiṭ
- Tabaqa: ninth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy, precise, devout" [ثقة متقن عابد, added]
- Critics' statements: Aḥmad — "a memoriser and precise" — (via al-Khulāṣah/Tahdhīb); al-ʿIjlī — "trustworthy and firm"; Abū Ḥātim — "He is an Imām; one does not ask about the likes of him"; al-Khulāṣah — one of the great famous memorisers
- Teachers mentioned: Sulaymān al-Taymī, Ḥumayd al-Ṭawīl, al-Jarīrī, Dāwūd ibn Abī Hind, many others
- Students mentioned: Baqiyyah, Ibn al-Madīnī, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd, many others
- Born / died: born 118 AH (Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, via al-Jamʿ); died in Wāsiṭ at the beginning of Rabīʿ al-Awwal 206 AH (Ibn Saʿd), nearly ninety (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: all six

- Kunya: Abū ʿĪsā
- Nisba(s): al-Shaybānī (tribal); al-Wāsiṭī

- Tabaqa: sixth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy, firm, virtuous" [ثقة ثبت فاضل, added]

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- One of the hadiths al-Dāraquṭnī criticised in al-Bukhārī (110 criticised in total: 78 al-Bukhārī alone, 32 shared with Muslim). The criticism: only al-ʿAwwām ibn Ḥawshab made it musnad; Misʿar narrated it from al-Saksakī from Abū Burdah as Abū Burdah's own words, without Abū Mūsā or the Prophet ﷺ. [scholar]
- Ibn Ḥajar's two answers (introduction to Fatḥ): Misʿar is more precise, but such a statement cannot be mere opinion, so it has the ruling of marfūʿ; and the travel story shows al-ʿAwwām preserved it (Aḥmad: a hadith containing a story indicates its narrator preserved it). [scholar]
- The teacher's two further answers: it is narrated connected and marfūʿ via another route in al-Adab al-Mufrad; and preferring the more precise narrator's mursal/mawqūf is only one view — many hold the ruling goes to the one who connects or raises, as an accepted addition of a trustworthy narrator (al-Nawawī, al-Taqrīb; al-Suyūṭī, al-Tadrīb; Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ, ʿUlūm al-Ḥadīth, citing al-Khaṭīb; al-Bukhārī on "no marriage except with a guardian" against Shuʿbah and Sufyān; al-ʿIrāqī's Alfiyyah). [scholar]
- All six narrators used by the six except Maṭar ibn al-Faḍl (al-Bukhārī only); Ibrāhīm al-Saksakī used otherwise only by Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī; al-Bukhārī has only two hadiths from him. [computable: rumuz]
- al-ʿAwwām is named without nasab; no ambiguity, he is the only al-ʿAwwām among al-Bukhārī's narrators (three in the six books). [computable: homonym check within the compiler's narrator set]
- A son narrating from his father: Abū Burdah from Abū Mūsā. [computable: father field]
- Two narrators from Wāsiṭ: Yazīd ibn Hārūn and his shaykh al-ʿAwwām ibn Ḥawshab. [computable: region]
- Yazīd ibn Abī Kabshah, the person in the story, appears nowhere else in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (Ibn Ḥajar). [computable if persons named in matns are indexed; otherwise scholar]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary / rhetoric notes:
  - al-laff wa al-nashr al-maqlūb (reversed distribution): illness and travel are mentioned first, then residence and health in reverse correspondence; Qur'anic example Āl ʿImrān 3:106–107; ordered type (murattab) example al-Jinn 72:14–15; Sunnah example "Two dead creatures and two bloods have been made lawful..."
  - Two kinds of attribution of a verb to its subject (Ibn Hishām, Shudhūr al-Dhahab): "travelled" — action proceeds from the servant; "became ill" — the state exists within him
- Commentaries cited: Fatḥ al-Bārī (al-Ismāʿīlī and al-Ṭabarānī corroborations; Yazīd ibn Abī Kabshah; the Dāraquṭnī defence); al-Nawawī, al-Suyūṭī, Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ, al-Khaṭīb, al-ʿIrāqī (on connected vs disconnected); Ibn Hishām (grammar)
- Meaning as stated: if illness prevents regular righteous deeds while the intention to continue remains, the reward is recorded; likewise for permissible travel. Placed in Jihād because jihād generally involves travel.
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. The early generations adhered to the Sunnah and guided others to it.
  2. Reviewing knowledge together and attending to evidences.
  3. The deeds of servants are recorded.
  4. One who regularly did good deeds and is prevented by illness receives their reward.
  5. A traveller receives the reward of what he did while resident.
  6. Allah's generosity: reward for good done and for good intended but prevented.
  7. Human weakness; a person never leaves the state of servanthood.
  8. Illness is a manifestation of human weakness.
  9. Travel is a human need; the best travel is for jihād (hence the placement).
  10. Refutation of those who say valid excuses only remove sin without securing the reward.
  11. Encouragement to remain consistent in obedience in health and residence.
