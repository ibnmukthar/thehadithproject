# Sahih al-Bukhari 3126

> Source: teacher's class notes, Google Doc "Twelfth Hadith" (the document supplied as "unknown"; course sequence number 12; the notes do not give the Bukhari number). Identified as Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com number **3126** [added], confirmed against the Arabic text of 3126 (isnad matches). The parallels are [added] 123 ('Ilm), 2810 (Jihad), 7458 (Tawhid). The notes are in English without diacritics for this document; spellings kept as given. Note: the notes render the first link "Muhammad ibn Bashshar narrated to us"; the Arabic text consulted reads حَدَّثَنِي ("narrated to me") — variant noted, not resolved.

## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: قَالَ أَعْرَابِيٌّ لِلنَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: الرَّجُلُ يُقَاتِلُ لِلْمَغْنَمِ، وَالرَّجُلُ يُقَاتِلُ لِيُذْكَرَ، وَيُقَاتِلُ لِيُرَى مَكَانُهُ، مَنْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ؟ فَقَالَ: «مَنْ قَاتَلَ لِتَكُونَ كَلِمَةُ اللَّهِ هِيَ الْعُلْيَا فَهْوَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ»
- English gist: Asked which of those who fight for spoils, for fame or for display is fighting in Allah's cause, the Prophet ﷺ said: whoever fights so that Allah's word is supreme.
- Kitab / bab: Book of the Obligation of the One-Fifth Share (Fard al-Khums), chapter "Does fighting for spoils diminish a person's reward?"

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. al-Bukhari | "narrated to us" (حدثنا; Arabic text consulted: حدثني) → Muhammad ibn Bashshar | Bundar; teacher of all six
2. Muhammad ibn Bashshar | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Ghundar | = Muhammad ibn Ja'far, by laqab here
3. Ghundar | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Shu'bah | Ghundar is the most reliable transmitter from Shu'bah
4. Shu'bah | "from" (عن) → 'Amr | = 'Amr ibn Murrah (named in full in Muslim's chain)
5. 'Amr ibn Murrah | "I heard" (سمعت) → Abu Wa'il | = Shaqiq ibn Salamah, by kunya here; Successor from Successor
6. Abu Wa'il | "Abu Musa al-Ash'ari narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Abu Musa | mukhadram narrating from a Companion
7. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari | "A Bedouin said to the Prophet ﷺ..." | the Bedouin is unnamed (possibly Lahiq ibn Dumayrah)

Six narrators between al-Bukhari and the Prophet ﷺ. The longer of al-Bukhari's two routes to Shu'bah (the Jihad route has Sulayman ibn Harb alone between them).

## Narrators

### Muhammad ibn Bashshar (Bundar)
- Full name and nasab: Muhammad ibn Bashshar ibn 'Uthman al-'Abdi al-Basri
- Kunya: Abu Bakr
- Laqab / title: Bundar — "a repository of hadith", having gathered the hadith of Basrah (al-Maqdisi)
- Nisba(s): al-'Abdi (tribal); al-Basri
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: tenth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Abu Bakr al-Basri — "The hadith master Bundar was one of the repositories of knowledge"; al-'Ijli, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Khuzaymah ("the imam of his age"), al-Firhayani, al-Dhuhli, Maslamah, Abu Hatim al-Razi — trustworthy — introduction to al-Fath; al-Dhahabi — "All the compilers of the Sahih collections relied upon him as an authority. He is unquestionably a reliable authority"; "one of the repositories of knowledge" — al-Mizan
- Teachers mentioned: Muhammad ibn Ja'far (Ghundar), Muhammad ibn Abi 'Adi, 'Abd al-Wahhab al-Thaqafi, others (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari and Muslim (extensively); the six compilers, Ibn Khuzaymah, Ibn Sa'id, many others (al-Khulasah)
- Born / died: born 167 AH, the year Hammad ibn Salamah died (al-Maqdisi); died 252 AH in his eighties (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: a direct teacher of all six compilers; well known by his laqab.

### Ghundar
- Full name and nasab: Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Hudhali
- Kunya: Abu 'Abd Allah
- Laqab / title: Ghundar; "a dealer in cotton cloth"
- Nisba(s): al-Hudhali (by clientage); al-Basri
- Mawla status: "their client" (client of Hudhayl)
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: ninth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy, with an accurate written record and a degree of inattentiveness" [ثقة صحيح الكتاب إلا أن فيه غفلة, added]
- Critics' statements: 'Ali ibn al-Madini — "I prefer him to 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi in narrations from Shu'bah" — introduction to al-Fath; Ibn al-Mubarak — "When people differ concerning narrations from Shu'bah, Ghundar's book is the judge between them" — introduction to al-Fath; Abu Hatim — "His narrations from anyone other than Shu'bah may be recorded, and cannot be relied upon as evidence" — introduction to al-Fath; Ibn Hajar — al-Bukhari has many of his narrations from Shu'bah plus one from Ma'mar and one from 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'id ibn Abi Hind, both corroborated — introduction to al-Fath; al-Dhahabi — "one of the firmly reliable and precise narrators, particularly in narrations from Shu'bah" — al-Mizan; Yahya ibn Ma'in (via al-Mizan) — most accurate written record of all; a group tried and failed to find an error in his book; "He had been fasting on alternate days for fifty years"
- Teachers mentioned: Shu'bah (kept his company about twenty years; was his stepson), others
- Students mentioned: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ishaq ibn Rahawayh, Yahya ibn Ma'in, 'Ali ibn al-Madini, Bundar
- Born / died: died 193 or 194 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: known by both laqab and name; named by laqab here, by name elsewhere in al-Bukhari and in Muslim's chain.

### Shu'bah ibn al-Hajjaj
- Biography "already discussed among the narrators in the chain of the tenth hadith" (not in this document). Given here: Basran; Ghundar's stepfather.

### 'Amr ibn Murrah
- Full name and nasab: 'Amr ibn Murrah ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Tariq al-Jamali (fathah on the jim and the mim) al-Muradi
- Kunya: Abu 'Abd Allah
- Nisba(s): al-Jamali, al-Muradi (tribal); al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufa; blind
- Tabaqa: fifth generation (Taqrib); "one of the firmly reliable narrators among the younger Successors"
- Taqrib grading line: "Trustworthy and devout in worship. He did not practise tadlis and was accused of holding the doctrine of irja'" [ثقة عابد كان لا يدلس ورمي بالإرجاء, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Hajar — agreement on his trustworthiness; some criticised him for irja'; Shu'bah: "He did not practise tadlis"; all six relied on him — introduction to al-Fath; Mis'ar — "He was the person in Kufah dearest to me and the most virtuous"; "'Amr was one of the wellsprings of truthfulness" — Tahdhib
- Born / died: died 118 AH, "an earlier date has also been given" (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: no tadlis (explicitly attested); accused of irja'.

### Abu Wa'il
- Full name and nasab: Shaqiq ibn Salamah al-Asadi
- Kunya: Abu Wa'il
- Nisba(s): al-Asadi (tribal); al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufa
- Tabaqa: mukhadram (lived in both eras); "one of the leading Successors" (al-Khulasah)
- Taqrib grading line: "A trustworthy mukhadram" [ثقة مخضرم, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Ma'in — "Trustworthy. The standing of someone like him is beyond question" — Tahdhib; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr — "They unanimously agreed that he was trustworthy" — Tahdhib
- Teachers mentioned: Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali, Mu'adh ibn Jabal, others (al-Khulasah)
- Students mentioned: al-Sha'bi, 'Amr ibn Murrah, Mughirah ibn Miqsam, Mansur, Zubayd (al-Khulasah)
- Born / died: died during the caliphate of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, aged one hundred (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: known by both kunya and name; by kunya here, by name (Shaqiq) in Muslim's other chains, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah.

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- All six narrators are used by all six compilers. [computable: rumuz]
- The upper half of the chain is Kufan (Abu Musa, Abu Wa'il, 'Amr ibn Murrah) and the lower half Basran (Shu'bah, Ghundar, Bundar). [computable: region]
- al-Bukhari's shaykh Muhammad ibn Bashshar is known by his laqab Bundar and was a direct teacher of all six compilers. [computable: name-form + shaykh-of-six flag]
- The shaykh's shaykh is named by laqab (Ghundar) here, by personal name (Muhammad ibn Ja'far) elsewhere in al-Bukhari and in Muslim's chain. [computable: name-form variants across loci]
- Abu Wa'il is named by kunya here, by name (Shaqiq ibn Salamah) in Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah; Ibn Hajar (commentary on Nukhbat al-Fikar) on the importance of knowing the kunyas of those known by name and the names of those known by kunya. [computable: kunya/name variants]
- A Successor narrating from a Successor: 'Amr ibn Murrah from Abu Wa'il. [computable: generation]
- A mukhadram in the chain (Abu Wa'il), stated in his biography. [computable: tabaqa field]
- al-Bukhari gives the longer route here (Bundar → Ghundar → Shu'bah) having given the shorter (Sulayman ibn Harb → Shu'bah) in Jihad, to show multiple routes to Shu'bah; the longer route's merit is that Ghundar is the most reliable transmitter from Shu'bah. Ibn Hajar made the same point on the Faith hadith "One form of wrongdoing is less serious than another", given via Abu al-Walid al-Tayalisi (short) and via Bishr ibn Khalid → Ghundar (long): "Ghundar is the most reliable narrator from Shu'bah. For this reason the author included his narration, having already included the hadith through the shorter chain." [computable: chain-length comparison across loci; scholar: purpose]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - "fights for spoils" — to obtain war booty
  - "fights to become renowned" — to be spoken of, famous for courage (other narrations: "out of courage")
  - "fights to display his prowess" — other narration: "to show off"; further narrations: "out of partisan zeal", "out of anger"; Ibn Hajar: the combined narrations give five motives (spoils, courage, show, partisan zeal, anger), each open to praise or blame, hence the answer neither affirmed nor denied
  - "the word of Allah" — Allah's call to Islam (Fath); explained by the Prophet's "I have been commanded to fight the people until they testify..."; "word" used for a complete statement, as in Al 'Imran 3:64
  - "sabil Allah" — generally everything Allah legislated (al-Nahl 16:125); specifically jihad, as here
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari (identity of the Bedouin: possibly Lahiq ibn Dumayrah al-Bahili, recorded by Abu Musa al-Madini via 'Ufayr ibn Ma'dan, weak chain; the five motives; sole vs incidental motive; al-Tabari: when the original motive is right, later incidentals do no harm — the majority view; Abu Umamah's hadith in Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i, good chain: "He will receive nothing" three times, "Allah accepts only deeds sincerely devoted to Him"; the five categories of intention; Ibn Abi Jamrah; uslub al-hakim — the wise response, shifting from the fighting to the fighter; Ibn Battal: he departed from the questioner's wording because anger and zeal may be for Allah); 'Abd Allah ibn Hawalah's report (Abu Dawud, sound chain: sent on foot to obtain spoils) as evidence that an incidental aim does not harm
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Consulting scholars about religious matters.
  2. Deeds earn reward through righteous intention (supports "Actions are judged by intentions").
  3. The merit of jihad is specific to those who fight so that Allah's word is supreme.
  4. Permissibility of asking about the underlying reason.
  5. Knowledge precedes action.
  6. Condemnation of greed for worldly gain and fighting for personal gratification.
  7. "Word" used for a complete statement.
  8. A good mufti's answer addresses the questioner's purpose and adds benefit.
