## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: كُنْتُ مَعَ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم فِي الْغَارِ، فَرَأَيْتُ آثَارَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ، قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، لَوْ أَنَّ أَحَدَهُمْ رَفَعَ قَدَمَهُ رَآنَا. قَالَ: «مَا ظَنُّكَ بِاثْنَيْنِ اللَّهُ ثَالِثُهُمَا»
- English gist: In the cave, Abu Bakr feared the pursuers would see them if one looked down; the Prophet ﷺ replied, "What do you think of two when Allah is the third with them?"
- Kitab / bab: Book of Tafsir, under Allah's statement "...the second of the two, when they were in the cave, and he said to his companion, 'Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us'" (al-Tawbah 9:40).

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. al-Bukhari | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Abdullah ibn Muhammad | al-Musnadi; muhmal name resolved by frequency of narration
2. Abdullah ibn Muhammad | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Habban | = Habban ibn Hilal (ha with fathah)
3. Habban | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Hammam | = Hammam ibn Yahya
4. Hammam | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Thabit | = Thabit al-Bunani
5. Thabit | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Anas | 
6. Anas | "Abu Bakr told me" (حدثني أبو بكر) → Abu Bakr | Companion from Companion
7. Abu Bakr al-Siddiq | reports the exchange and the Prophet's ﷺ words | 

Six narrators between al-Bukhari and the Prophet ﷺ; musalsal with "haddathana". This is the lower (nazil) of al-Bukhari's three routes: two intermediaries between him and Hammam.

## Narrators

### Abdullah ibn Muhammad (al-Musnadi)
- Full name and nasab: Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Ja'far ibn al-Yaman
- Kunya: Abu Ja'far
- Laqab / title: al-Musnadi (nun with fathah) — because he devoted himself to collecting musnad (connected) narrations and cared little for maqtu' or mursal (al-Maqdisi); al-Hakim: because he was the first to compile a Musnad of the Companions beyond the Oxus
- Nisba(s): al-Ju'fi (tribal); al-Bukhari (geographic)
- City / region: Bukhara (seen in Wasit by Ahmad ibn Sayyar; returned to Bukhara and died there)
- Tabaqa: tenth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy (thiqah) memoriser (hafiz); he compiled the Musnad" [ثقة حافظ, added]
- Critics' statements: Abu Hatim — "truthful (saduq)" — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib; Ibn Hibban — in al-Thiqat, "he was precise" — Tahdhib; Ahmad ibn Sayyar — integrity, truthfulness, man of the Sunnah, precision; tall, white hair and beard — Tahdhib; al-Hakim — leading hadith scholar of his era in that region; al-Khalili — "trustworthy, and there is consensus regarding him"
- Teachers mentioned: Sufyan ibn Uyaynah, Yahya ibn Adam, Harami ibn Umarah, many others (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari (numerous places)
- Born / died: died 229 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: al-Bukhari and al-Tirmidhi
- Notable: his grandfather al-Yaman al-Ju'fi was the one through whom al-Bukhari's grandfather al-Mughirah ibn Bardizbah embraced Islam, hence al-Bukhari's nisba al-Ju'fi. "Abdullah ibn Muhammad" unqualified in al-Bukhari means al-Ju'fi, not Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shaybah (Ibn Hajar) — a muhmal resolved by frequency. Ibn Hajar: among the most distinguished of al-Bukhari's teachers though al-Bukhari met others with shorter chains.

### Habban ibn Hilal
- Full name and nasab: Habban (ha with fathah) ibn Hilal al-Bahili, also said al-Kinani
- Kunya: Abu Habib
- Nisba(s): al-Bahili / al-Kinani (tribal, disputed)
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: ninth generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy and exceptionally reliable (thiqah thabt)" [ثقة ثبت, added]
- Critics' statements: Ahmad ibn Hanbal — "In Basrah, the highest level of precision ended with him" — Tahdhib; declared trustworthy by Yahya ibn Ma'in, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Sa'd, al-'Ijli, al-Bazzar, al-Khatib — Tahdhib
- Teachers mentioned: Hammam ibn Yahya (in both Sahihs), numerous others (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: Ahmad, al-Darimi, Abdullah al-Musnadi, Yahya ibn Muhammad ibn al-Sakan, Ali ibn Muslim (al-Maqdisi)
- Born / died: died 216 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: not to be confused with Hibban (kasrah) ibn Musa, who never met Hammam (introduction to Fath); the most precise of the three who transmitted this hadith from Hammam.

### Hammam ibn Yahya
- Full name and nasab: Hammam ibn Yahya ibn Dinar al-Azdi al-'Awdhi al-Muhallami
- Kunya: Abu Abdullah; also said Abu Bakr
- Nisba(s): al-Azdi, al-'Awdhi, al-Muhallami (tribal, by clientage)
- Mawla status: "the freedman of his tribe"
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: seventh generation (Taqrib)
- Taqrib grading line: "Trustworthy. He occasionally made mistakes" [ثقة ربما وهم, added]
- Critics' statements: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Yahya ibn Ma'in, Abu Hatim, al-'Ijli, al-Hakim — declared him trustworthy — Tahdhib; Ibn Hajar — "one of the firmly established authorities", "some criticism was directed at a number of the narrations he transmitted from memory" — introduction to Fath al-Bari; al-Dhahabi — "one of the scholars and trustworthy authorities of Basrah" — Mizan al-I'tidal
- Teachers mentioned: Ata ibn Abi Rabah, Ishaq ibn Abi Talhah, Zayd ibn Aslam, Thabit al-Bunani, many others
- Students mentioned: Sufyan al-Thawri (a contemporary), Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn 'Ulayyah, Waki', Ibn Mahdi, Habban ibn Hilal, Yazid ibn Harun, many others
- Born / died: died 164 or 165 AH (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: madar of this hadith from Thabit (al-Tirmidhi, al-Bazzar), with Ja'far ibn Sulayman's route in Ibn Shahin's al-Afrad as corroboration; "Hammam" unqualified is unambiguous — the other two Hammams in al-Bukhari (Hammam ibn al-Harith, Hammam ibn Munabbih) are not of his generation.

### Thabit al-Bunani
- Full name: Thabit ibn Aslam al-Bunani (as identified in the subtleties)
- City / region: Basra; remained in Anas's company forty years (Tahdhib)
- Biography: "already introduced in the discussion of the sixth hadith" (not in this document)

### Anas ibn Malik (Companion)
- The servant of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; Basran; the only Companion named Anas among al-Bukhari's narrators
- Biography: "already introduced in the discussion of the sixth hadith" (not in this document)

### Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (Companion)
- Full name and nasab: Abdullah ibn Uthman ibn Amir ibn Amr ibn Ka'b ibn Sa'd ibn Taym ibn Murrah [ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib] al-Taymi; "Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafah"; lineage meets the Prophet's ﷺ at Murrah ibn Ka'b with the same number of forefathers (Ibn Hajar)
- Kunya: Abu Bakr (famous by kunya, as was his father Abu Quhafah)
- Laqab / title: al-Siddiq al-Akbar; Atiq ("the title Atiq became more widely known" — Aishah via Sa'id ibn Mansur). Explanations of Atiq (Fath, opening of Virtues of the Companions): original name; nobility of lineage free of blemish; precedence in goodness and early Islam; handsome appearance; his mother's words at birth "O Allah, this is Your freed one from death" (none of her previous children survived); the Prophet's glad tidings that Allah freed him from the Fire — the last supported by Aishah's hadith (al-Tirmidhi) and Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr's (al-Bazzar, authenticated by Ibn Hibban) adding "Before that his name had been Abdullah ibn Uthman". Al-Siddiq: for immediate, unwavering affirmation of the Prophet ﷺ; or first so known the morning after al-Isra'; Ali used to swear Allah bestowed the title from above the heavens (al-Tabarani, narrators trustworthy; al-Daraqutni, al-Afrad, via Abu Ishaq from Abu Yahya: "Allah named Abu Bakr al-Siddiq upon the tongue of His Prophet")
- Nisba(s): al-Taymi
- Father: Uthman ibn Abi Quhafah (no disagreement on the name or the kunya); mother: Salma, Umm al-Khayr, daughter of Sakhr ibn Amir ibn Amr; both parents and all his children accepted Islam; his father narrated from the Prophet ﷺ
- Companion facts (al-Isabah): born two years and six months after the Year of the Elephant; accompanied the Prophet ﷺ before revelation; first adult male to believe; constantly with him in Makkah; accompanied him in the migration and the cave; present in every expedition; carried the banner at Tabuk; led the Hajj in the ninth year; the successor (khalifah) of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Ibn Ishaq (Sirah): beloved and approachable, greatest genealogist of Quraysh, merchant; through him Uthman, Talhah, al-Zubayr, Sa'd and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf accepted Islam
- Students mentioned (al-Khulasah): "his children Abd al-Rahman, Aishah, Muhammad, Umar, Ali, and many others" (as given; "Umar, Ali" listed among his children appears garbled — unclear in notes)
- Hadith counts: 142 total (al-Khazraji, al-Khulasah); agreed 6; al-Bukhari alone 11; Muslim alone 1; Ibn Hajar (introduction to Fath): 22 in al-Bukhari
- Died: Jumada al-Ula 13 AH, aged 63 (Taqrib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: Ibn Asakir's biography of him is about one volume, roughly one-eightieth of Tarikh Dimashq; Ibn Kathir (al-Bidayah wa al-Nihayah) devoted a separate volume. "His companion" in 9:40 is Abu Bakr without disagreement.

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- All six narrators are used by the six compilers except Abdullah ibn Muhammad, used otherwise only by al-Tirmidhi. [computable: rumuz]
- The chain is musalsal by the formula "haddathana" throughout (Ibn Hajar, Nukhbat al-Fikar: agreement in transmission formula or another characteristic makes a chain musalsal). [computable: formula]
- Four consecutive Basrans: Habban, Hammam, Thabit, Anas. [computable: region]
- Two Companions in the chain: a Companion narrating from a Companion (Anas from Abu Bakr). [computable: generation]
- Al-Musnadi was one of al-Bukhari's principal teachers; his grandfather al-Yaman al-Ju'fi brought al-Bukhari's grandfather al-Mughirah ibn Bardizbah to Islam, hence al-Bukhari's nisba al-Ju'fi. [scholar]
- The laqab al-Musnadi records his method: collecting musnad reports rather than mursal or interrupted ones. [scholar; name-form field]
- "Abdullah ibn Muhammad" is a muhmal (several teachers of al-Bukhari bear the name, including Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shaybah); unqualified it means al-Ju'fi because al-Bukhari narrates from him most (Ibn Hajar). [computable: homonym detection; scholar: resolution rule]
- "Habban" without nasab is unambiguous — no other Habban (fathah) among al-Bukhari's narrators; cannot be Hibban (kasrah) ibn Musa, who never met Hammam (introduction to Fath). Muslim gives the full name. [computable: homonym + teacher-student overlap]
- "Hammam" without nasab is unambiguous — the two other Hammams in al-Bukhari are not of his generation. [computable: homonym + generation]
- "Thabit" = al-Bunani, though other Tabi'un named Thabit narrate from Anas, because both are Basran and he kept Anas's company forty years. [scholar; partly computable from region + teacher link]
- "Anas" unqualified — no other Companion Anas among al-Bukhari's narrators; Muslim gives the nasab. [computable]
- This route is nazil (two between al-Bukhari and Hammam) while the Fada'il and Hijrah routes are 'ali (one); the purpose may be to show the multiple routes to Hammam and that all three transmitters from him — Habban ibn Hilal, Muhammad ibn Sinan, Musa ibn Isma'il — were Basran; Habban was the most precise (Ahmad). [computable: chain-length comparison across loci + region; scholar: purpose]
- The Companion narrator is the best of mankind after the Prophets and Messengers; famous by kunya like his father (Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafah); personal name Abdullah, father Uthman; both parents and all children Muslims. [scholar]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary / grammar notes:
  - "the cave" — the cave on Mount Thawr, during the Hijrah
  - "law" (if) — Ibn Hajar: evidence that law may refer to the future, against the majority (cf. al-Hujurat 49:7); on that view Abu Bakr spoke while the polytheists stood above; on the majority view, after they left, in gratitude
  - "Allah is the third with them" — Ibn Hajar: His support and help, since by knowledge He is with every two; al-Nawawi (Sharh Muslim): support, assistance, protection and guidance
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari (parallel reports; law; meaning of ma'iyyah; narrators of Ibn Abbas report trustworthy); al-Nawawi, Sharh Sahih Muslim; al-Isabah (companionship in the cave shared by no one else); supporting reports: Aishah (al-Bukhari, Hijrah: three nights in the cave on Thawr), Abu Sa'id (Ibn Hibban: "You are my brother and my companion in the cave"), Ibn Abbas with Ibn al-Zubayr (al-Bukhari, Tafsir: "his grandfather was the companion of the cave"), Ibn Abbas via Amr ibn Maymun (Ahmad, al-Hakim: stoning of Ali; Abu Bakr goes to the well of Maymun and enters the cave), Abdullah ibn Ahmad's additions to the Musnad (Ibn Abbas: "Abu Bakr is my companion and my source of comfort in the cave")
- Qur'an cited: al-Tawbah 9:40; al-Hujurat 49:7; al-Nahl 16:128; al-Mujadilah 58:7; al-'Ankabut 29:69; al-Hadid 57:4
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. The immense virtue of Abu Bakr (al-Nawawi: among his greatest virtues): the Prophet's words, his self-sacrifice, leaving his family, constant companionship, enduring hostility, shielding the Prophet.
  2. The Prophet's ﷺ complete tawakkul in Allah.
  3. The intensity of the polytheists' hostility and intent to kill him.
  4. Taking appropriate means for safety (hiding in the cave).
  5. Travelling with a companion, choosing one of virtue.
  6. Comforting and reassuring one's travelling companion.
  7. Consolation and patience for those who follow the Prophet's path in conveying Islam.
  8. The caller to truth should expect harm ("Paradise is surrounded by hardships...").
  9. The final outcome is for those who fear Allah; He aids His righteous servants.
  10. Abu Bakr's manners: "I was with the Prophet", the word "with" expressing followership.
  11. Permissible to relate what happens to oneself and companions on travel if nothing requires confidentiality.
  12. Allah's ma'iyyah: general (knowledge, 58:7) and special (support of Messengers and the righteous: 9:40, 16:128, 29:69), not contradicting His exaltedness above the Throne (57:4 combines both).
