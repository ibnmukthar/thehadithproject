# Sahih al-Bukhari 1597

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 8" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Hadith Eight", ID `10IxEBLt6afsOWyjuc13gR5eP4U396EQolycpeX9RLuQ`). The notes give no Fath al-Bari number. [added] Identified as al-Bukhari **1597** (Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering; Kitab al-Hajj, bab ma dhukira fi al-hajar al-aswad) from the chain and chapter stated in the notes.

## Text
- Arabic matn: not given in the notes (English only). [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:1597] عَنْ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّهُ جَاءَ إِلَى الْحَجَرِ الأَسْوَدِ فَقَبَّلَهُ، فَقَالَ: إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ حَجَرٌ لاَ تَضُرُّ وَلاَ تَنْفَعُ، وَلَوْلاَ أَنِّي رَأَيْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يُقَبِّلُكَ مَا قَبَّلْتُكَ
- English as rendered: ʿUmar came to the Black Stone and kissed it, then said: "I know that you are only a stone. You can neither harm nor benefit. Had I not seen the Prophet ﷺ kissing you, I would not have kissed you."
- Gist: ʿUmar kisses the Black Stone and publicly declares he does so only because he saw the Prophet ﷺ do it, the stone itself having no power to harm or benefit.
- Kitab / bab: Kitab al-Hajj, chapter "What Has Been Mentioned Regarding the Black Stone" (the only hadith in that chapter)

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. Muhammad ibn Kathir | ḥaddathanā, qāla | Basran; al-Bukhārī's shaykh
2. Sufyan [al-Thawri] | akhbaranā ("informed us") | Kufan; mudallis; no route found in which he states explicit hearing
3. al-Aʿmash [Sulayman ibn Mihran] | ʿan | Kufan; mudallis; explicit hearing ("Ibrahim narrated to us") recorded in Musnad Aḥmad
4. Ibrahim [al-Nakhaʿi] | ʿan | Kufan; known for irsāl
5. ʿAbis ibn Rabiʿah | ʿan | Kufan; mukhaḍram
6. ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab | ʿan ... annahu ("that he came...") | Companion. The opening words are ʿAbis's description of ʿUmar's act, not ʿUmar's own statement (lata'if point 10)

Mawqūf in form (ʿUmar's act and words), but containing ʿUmar's report of the Prophet's ﷺ action.

## Narrators

### Muhammad ibn Kathir
- Full name and nasab: Muhammad ibn Kathir al-ʿAbdi al-Basri; brother of Sulayman [ibn Kathir]
- Kunya: Abu ʿAbdullah
- Nisba(s): al-ʿAbdi; al-Basri
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: "senior scholars of the tenth generation" (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy (thiqah). Those who criticised him were mistaken"; died 223 AH aged ninety
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib):
  - Yahya ibn Maʿin — "He was not trustworthy"
  - Ahmad ibn Hanbal — trustworthy
  - Abu Hatim — truthful (ṣaduq)
  - Ibn Hibban — included among the trustworthy
- Teachers mentioned: his brother Sulayman, Shuʿbah, al-Thawri (al-Maqdisi, al-Jamʿ)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari (several places); Muslim one hadith via ʿAbdullah al-Darimi from him from his brother, concerning dreams (al-Maqdisi)
- Died: 223 AH, aged 90
- Rumuz: all six

### Sufyan al-Thawri
- Identification: "the Sufyan here is Sufyan al-Thawri" (Ibn Ḥajar, Fath al-Bari)
- Full name and nasab: Sufyan ibn Saʿid ibn Masruq al-Thawri
- Kunya: Abu ʿAbdullah
- Laqab / title: "Amīr al-Muʾminīn fī al-ḥadīth" (Shuʿbah, Ibn ʿUyaynah, Abu ʿAsim, Ibn Maʿin and others); one of "the two Sufyans" (with Sufyan ibn ʿUyaynah)
- Nisba(s): al-Thawri; al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufah
- Tabaqa: "foremost of the seventh generation" (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): trustworthy, leading ḥāfiẕ, jurist, devout worshipper, imām, ḥujjah; "occasionally practised tadlīs"; died 161 AH aged 64
- Critics' statements:
  - Shuʿbah, Ibn ʿUyaynah, Abu ʿAsim, Ibn Maʿin and others — "Sufyan is the Commander of the Faithful in Hadith" — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib
  - Ibn al-Mubarak — "I wrote from eleven hundred teachers, and I did not write from anyone better than Sufyan" — Tahdhib
  - al-Khatib — one of the imams of the Muslims; leadership unanimously acknowledged; combined precision, memorisation, knowledge, accuracy, piety, asceticism — Tahdhib
  - al-Nasa'i — "greater than merely being described as trustworthy; one of the Imams concerning whom I hope Allah has made an example for the righteous" — Tahdhib
  - al-Dhahabi — established authority, reliable imām by consensus, "although he did practise tadlīs from weak narrators", yet with critical insight; the claim "he practised tadlīs and narrated from liars carries no weight" — Mizan al-Iʿtidal
- Teachers mentioned: his father, Abu Ishaq al-Shaybani, Abu Ishaq al-Sabiʿi, ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿUmayr, al-Aʿmash
- Students mentioned: al-Awzaʿi, Malik, Misʿar (contemporary), "Ibn al-Mahdi" (so in notes), Ibn al-Mubarak
- Died: 161 AH, aged 64
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: tadlīs; no route found where he states explicit hearing of this hadith

### al-Aʿmash
- Full name and nasab: Sulayman ibn Mihran al-Asadi al-Kahili
- Kunya: Abu Muhammad
- Laqab / title: al-Aʿmash (laqab); known by both laqab and name
- Nisba(s): al-Asadi al-Kahili
- City / region: Kufah (lata'if)
- Tabaqa: fifth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): trustworthy, outstanding memoriser, expert in Qurʾānic recitation, pious, "however, he practised tadlīs"
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Maʿin, al-Nasa'i, al-ʿIjli — trustworthy — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib
  - Ibn Hibban — among the trustworthy Tabiʿun — Tahdhib
  - al-Dhahabi — "One of the firmly established Imams. Nothing was criticised about him except his tadlīs"; sometimes did tadlīs from weak narrators unrecognised; "he narrated to us" is unproblematic, "from" leaves the possibility of tadlīs, except from teachers he narrated from extensively — Ibrahim, "Ibn Abi Wa'il" (so in notes), Abu Salih al-Samman — whose narrations are regarded as connected — Mizan al-Iʿtidal
- Teachers mentioned: ʿAmir al-Shaʿbi, Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi, Mujahid ibn Jabr
- Students mentioned: Shuʿbah, the two Sufyans, Ibn al-Mubarak, Hushaym
- Born / died: born 61 AH; died 147 or 148 AH
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: tadlīs; explicit hearing from Ibrahim for this hadith in Musnad Ahmad

### Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi
- Identification: "the Ibrahim in this chain is Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi" (Fath al-Bari)
- Full name and nasab: Ibrahim ibn Yazid ibn Qays ibn al-Aswad ibn ʿAmr ibn Rabiʿah ibn Dhuhl al-Nakhaʿi
- Kunya: Abu ʿImran
- Laqab / title: leading jurist (of the Tabiʿun)
- Nisba(s): al-Nakhaʿi; al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufah
- Tabaqa: not stated (Tabiʿi)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "Trustworthy, although he frequently narrated mursal reports. He died in 96 AH at about fifty years of age"
- Critics' statements:
  - al-Hafiz Abu Saʿid al-ʿAla'i — "He frequently narrated mursal reports" — (via Tahdhib)
  - al-Bayhaqi — regarded his mursal reports from Ibn Masʿud as authentic; "a number of Imams accepted his mursal narrations" — (via Tahdhib)
  - al-Dhahabi — one of the great scholars; narrated mursal from many; saw Zayd ibn Arqam and others, but direct hearing from any Companion not established; he himself is authoritative but his mursal reports (from Ibn Masʿud or anyone) are not — Mizan al-Iʿtidal
- Teachers mentioned: his maternal uncles al-Aswad and ʿAbd al-Rahman, sons of Yazid; Masruq; ʿAlqamah; Ibn Maʿmar; Hammam ibn al-Harith; Shurayh the judge; Sahm ibn Minjab; from ʿA'ishah (hearing not established)
- Students mentioned: al-Aʿmash, Mansur, Ibn ʿAwn, Zubayd al-Yami, Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman, al-Mughirah ibn Miqsam
- Died: 96 AH, aged about 50
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: irsāl; first in Islam recorded using the expression "that which has no flowing blood" (مَا لَيْسَ لَهُ نَفْسٌ سَائِلَةٌ) for flies etc., adopted by the jurists after him (Ibn al-Qayyim, Zad al-Maʿad, on the fly hadith)

### ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab
- Full name and nasab: ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab ibn Nufayl ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzza ibn Riyah ibn ʿAbdullah ibn Qurt ibn Razah ibn ʿAdi ibn Kaʿb al-Qurashi al-ʿAdawi (Taqrīb)
- Kunya: Abu Hafs (al-Khulasah)
- Laqab / title: Amir al-Mu'minin — the first to be so called; al-Faruq; second Rightly Guided Caliph; one of the ten promised Paradise; one of the jurists among the Companions
- Nisba(s): al-Qurashi al-ʿAdawi; al-Madani
- Islam / hijra / battles: accepted Islam after forty men; Badr and all the major campaigns; led the community after Abu Bakr; many conquests in his caliphate (al-Khulasah)
- Narrated from: the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb (Tahdhib)
- Students mentioned: his children ʿAbdullah, ʿAsim, ʿUbaydullah and Hafsah; ʿUthman; ʿAli; ʿAlqamah ibn Waqqas; many Companions and Tabiʿun
- Hadith counts (al-Khulasah): 539 total; 10 agreed; 9 al-Bukhari only; 15 Muslim only. Number in al-Bukhari: 60 (Ibn Ḥajar, introduction to Fath al-Bari)
- Died: martyred Dhul-Hijjah 23 AH after ten and a half years as caliph (Taqrīb); "martyred at the end of 23 AH and buried at the beginning of 24 AH at the age of sixty-three"; Suhayb led the funeral prayer; buried in the Prophet's chamber (al-Khulasah)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: Ibn ʿUmar's hadith "Allah has placed the truth upon the tongue and in the heart of ʿUmar"; Ibn Masʿud at his burial: "Today nine-tenths of knowledge has departed"; long biographies by Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Dhahabi (his history) and Ibn Kathir (al-Bidayah wa al-Nihayah, who also compiled his legal narrations by fiqh chapter); al-Muhibb al-Tabari, al-Riyad al-Nadirah, about fifty pages

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All six narrators are used by all six compilers. [computable]
2. Three Tabiʿun narrating from one another: al-Aʿmash ← Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi ← ʿAbis ibn Rabiʿah. [computable]
3. Four consecutive Kufans: Sufyan al-Thawri, al-Aʿmash, Ibrahim, ʿAbis. [computable]
4. Two narrators of the Nakhaʿ tribe: Ibrahim and ʿAbis. [computable]
5. Two mudallisūn: Sufyan al-Thawri and al-Aʿmash; al-Aʿmash's explicit hearing is in Musnad Ahmad; the teacher found no route in which Sufyan states explicit hearing. [computable: tadlīs flags; locating the explicit-hearing route is computable with route data, the judgement is scholar]
6. Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi is among the Tabiʿun known for mursal reports, and one of their jurists; originator of the phrase "that which has no flowing blood" (Ibn al-Qayyim, Zad al-Maʿad). [computable flag / scholar]
7. The chain contains an "Amīr al-Muʾminīn fī al-ḥadīth": Sufyan al-Thawri (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib). [computable title]
8. A mukhaḍram: ʿAbis ibn Rabiʿah — lived in Jāhiliyyah and Islam without seeing the Prophet ﷺ; counted among senior Tabiʿun. [computable]
9. al-Aʿmash is a laqab for Sulayman ibn Mihran; knowing this prevents counting one man as two narrators. [computable]
10. The opening wording ("that he came to the Black Stone and kissed it") is ʿAbis's statement about ʿUmar, not ʿUmar's own words; al-Mundhiri's abridgement of Sunan Abi Dawud words it "From ʿAbis ibn Rabiʿah, from ʿUmar, that he came to the Stone..." [scholar]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - "neither harms nor benefits" — i.e. except by Allah's permission (Ibn Ḥajar)
  - "I know that you are only a stone..." — addressed to the Stone so that those present would hear: "I am addressing you, while intending that my neighbour should hear"
  - Emphasis: ʿUmar opens with *inna*; some versions add an oath and the emphatic *lām*
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari — the chapter has only this hadith because no other on the Black Stone met al-Bukhārī's conditions; other Black Stone hadiths: Ibn ʿAbbas "descended from Paradise whiter than milk; the sins of the children of Adam blackened it" (al-Tirmidhi, graded ṣaḥīḥ; chain has ʿAta' ibn al-Sa'ib whose memory later became confused, Jarir heard after that; strengthened by another chain in Sahih Ibn Khuzaymah); al-Nasa'i via Hammad ibn Salamah from ʿAta' (heard before the confusion) "The Black Stone is from Paradise"; Ibn Khuzaymah's Ibn ʿAbbas hadith "This Stone has a tongue and two lips; it will testify for those who touched it" (graded authentic by Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim; supported by Anas in al-Hakim); al-Hakim from Abu Saʿid: ʿAli replied to ʿUmar "It does harm and benefit" and mentioned the covenant written on a parchment inside the Stone, with the Prophet's ﷺ saying that the Stone will testify with an eloquent tongue — its chain has Abu Harun al-ʿAbdi, extremely weak; al-Shawkani, Nayl al-Awtar: strengthened by the Ibn ʿAbbas hadith. Al-Tabari (via Fath al-Bari): ʿUmar spoke because people had only recently left idol-worship and might think touching the Stone venerated stones as in Jāhiliyyah; he taught that it is done in obedience to the Prophet's ﷺ practice.
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Kissing the Black Stone is prescribed.
  2. Kissing anything whose kissing is not prescribed by the Shariʿah is prohibited.
  3. The Sunnah is explained through both words and actions.
  4. The Prophet's ﷺ actions are legislation for the Ummah unless evidence shows an action exclusive to him.
  5. Obligation to submit to the Lawgiver and rely on what is authentically established.
  6. Submission does not depend on knowing the wisdom behind every command.
  7. The Black Stone has no inherent power to benefit or harm.
  8. A ruler or scholar who fears a prescribed act may be misunderstood should promptly explain.
  9. ʿUmar's complete sincerity and concern for the Muslims.
  10. Speech may be emphasised when the audience requires it (*inna*, oath, *lām*).
  11. Great care in explaining the truth, especially in creed.
  12. The wisdom of al-Faruq in explaining the Sunnah and Allah's support for him — confirming the hadith "Among the nations before you were people who were inspired (muḥaddathūn); if there is any in my Ummah, it is ʿUmar" (al-Bukhari).
