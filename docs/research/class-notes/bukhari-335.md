# Sahih al-Bukhari 335

> Source: teacher's class notes, Google Doc "Hadith Three" (course sequence number 3; the notes do not give the Bukhari number). Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com number **335** is [added], confirmed against the Arabic text of 335 (isnad matches the notes' chain exactly). The notes are in English; Arabic isnad/matn below is [added] from the sunnah.com-numbered text; Arabic grading terms in brackets are [added] reconstructions of the Taqrib's standard wording.

## Text
- Arabic matn [added]: أُعْطِيتُ خَمْسًا لَمْ يُعْطَهُنَّ أَحَدٌ قَبْلِي: نُصِرْتُ بِالرُّعْبِ مَسِيرَةَ شَهْرٍ، وَجُعِلَتْ لِيَ الأَرْضُ مَسْجِدًا وَطَهُورًا، فَأَيُّمَا رَجُلٍ مِنْ أُمَّتِي أَدْرَكَتْهُ الصَّلاَةُ فَلْيُصَلِّ، وَأُحِلَّتْ لِيَ الْمَغَانِمُ وَلَمْ تَحِلَّ لأَحَدٍ قَبْلِي، وَأُعْطِيتُ الشَّفَاعَةَ، وَكَانَ النَّبِيُّ يُبْعَثُ إِلَى قَوْمِهِ خَاصَّةً، وَبُعِثْتُ إِلَى النَّاسِ عَامَّةً
- English gist: The Prophet ﷺ lists five things given to him and to no one before him: victory by awe a month's journey ahead, the earth as a place of prayer and a purifier, lawful war spoils, intercession, and a mission to all mankind rather than one people.
- Kitab / bab: Book of Tayammum, "at the beginning" of the book (bab heading not given in notes). Also repeated in the Book of Prayer and (partially) the Book of the Obligation of Khums.

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. al-Bukhārī | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Muḥammad ibn Sinān; and, after ح, "narrated to me" (حدثني) → Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr | two shaykhs, heard separately; plural vs singular formula noted by Ibn Ḥajar
2. Muḥammad ibn Sinān | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Hushaym | 
3. ح (chain switch) | | al-Bukhārī rarely uses ح; Muslim often
4. Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr | "informed us" (أخبرنا) → Hushaym | Ibn Ḥajar: Saʿīd probably read to Hushaym or heard it read (ʿarḍ), hence akhbaranā
5. Hushaym | "informed us" (أخبرنا) → Sayyār | Hushaym is a known mudallis; explicit transmission here
6. Sayyār | "narrated to us" (حدثنا) → Yazīd ("he is Ibn Ṣuhayb al-Faqīr") | in-isnad identification of Yazīd
7. Yazīd al-Faqīr | "informed us" (أخبرنا) → Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh | 
8. Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh | "that the Prophet ﷺ said" (أن النبي ﷺ قال) | 

The wording (lafz) is Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr's (Ibn Ḥajar, Fatḥ). Five narrators between al-Bukhārī and the Prophet ﷺ on either route.

Ibn Ḥajar (Fatḥ): the central narrator (madār) of Jābir's hadith is Hushaym with this chain.

## Narrators

### Muḥammad ibn Sinān
- Full name and nasab: Muḥammad ibn Sinān al-Bāhilī
- Kunya: Abū Bakr
- Laqab / title: known as al-ʿAwqī
- Nisba(s): al-Bāhilī (tribal); al-Baṣrī (geographic); al-ʿAwqī (al-ʿAwqah, a clan of al-Azd, among whom he settled; attribution by residence, not descent)
- Mawla status: not stated
- City / region: Basra
- Tabaqa: senior scholars of the tenth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy and firm" [ثقة ثبت, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Maʿīn — declared him trustworthy — Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb; al-Dāraquṭnī — declared him trustworthy — Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb; "a number of scholars" affirmed his reliability
- Teachers mentioned: Ibrāhīm ibn Ṭahmān, Fulayḥ, Hushaym, others
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhārī, Abū Dāwūd, others
- Born / died: died 223 AH (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: al-Bukhārī, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, al-Tirmidhī (Taqrīb). Not used by Muslim or al-Nasāʾī.
- Notable: nisba al-ʿAwqī is a "misleading attribution" (Nukhbat al-Fikar category): by residence among al-ʿAwqah, not lineage.

### Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr
- Full name and nasab: Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr al-Baghdādī
- Kunya: Abū ʿUthmān
- Nisba(s): al-Baghdādī; resided in Āmul of Jayḥūn
- City / region: Baghdad origin; Āmul (on the Jayḥūn/Oxus)
- Tabaqa: tenth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Ḥibbān — included him among the trustworthy (al-Thiqāt) — via Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb
- Teachers mentioned: Hushaym, ʿUthmān ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Waqqāṣī, others
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhārī, al-Faḍl ibn Aḥmad ibn Sahl al-Āmulī
- Born / died: died 234 AH (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: al-Bukhārī only
- Notable: al-Bukhārī heard this from him alone ("narrated to me").

### Hushaym
- Full name and nasab: Hushaym (diminutive form) ibn Bashīr ibn al-Qāsim ibn Dīnār al-Sulamī; "Abū Muʿāwiyah ibn Abī Khāzim" (his father Bashīr's kunya Abū Khāzim)
- Kunya: Abū Muʿāwiyah
- Nisba(s): al-Sulamī (with ḍammah, Banū Sulaym); al-Wāsiṭī; origin from Balkh; grandfather al-Qāsim settled in Wāsiṭ for trade (al-Maqdisī)
- Mawla status: "their freedman" (mawla of Banū Sulaym) — al-Maqdisī
- City / region: Wāsiṭ
- Tabaqa: seventh generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy and firm, though he practised frequent tadlīs and subtle irsāl" [ثقة ثبت كثير التدليس والإرسال الخفي, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Ḥajar — one of the leading imams whose reliability is agreed upon, known for tadlīs; al-Bukhārī took from him only reports where he stated direct transmission, found either in the chain itself or in a supporting chain; none of his narrations from al-Zuhrī are in the two Ṣaḥīḥs — introduction to Fatḥ al-Bārī; al-ʿIrāqī — listed him among the mudallisīn in the Ṣaḥīḥ ("In the Ṣaḥīḥ are several such as al-Aʿmash … and after him Hushaym, so investigate") — Alfiyyah
- Teachers mentioned: Ḥumayd al-Ṭawīl, Sayyār Abū al-Ḥakam, Khālid al-Ḥadhdhāʾ, al-Aʿmash (Tahdhīb)
- Students mentioned: Ibn al-Mubārak, Wakīʿ, Yazīd ibn Hārūn (Tahdhīb)
- Born / died: died 183 AH, approaching eighty (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: tadlīs and subtle irsāl; explicit transmission (akhbaranā) in this chain; madār of this hadith.

### Sayyār
- Full name and nasab: Sayyār ibn Abī Sayyār; his father's name Wardān
- Kunya: Abū al-Ḥakam
- Nisba(s): al-ʿAnazī (tribal); al-Wāsiṭī
- City / region: Wāsiṭ
- Tabaqa: sixth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Aḥmad, Ibn Maʿīn, al-Nasāʾī — declared him trustworthy — via Ibn Ḥajar (Tahdhīb)
- Teachers mentioned: al-Shaʿbī, Thābit al-Bunānī, Yazīd al-Faqīr (in both Ṣaḥīḥs); Sulaymān al-Ashjaʿī (in al-Bukhārī) — al-Maqdisī
- Students mentioned: Shuʿbah, Hushaym (both Ṣaḥīḥs); Qurrah ibn Khālid (Muslim) — al-Maqdisī
- Born / died: died 122 AH (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: said to be the brother of Musāwir al-Warrāq; appears by kunya "Abū al-Ḥakam" in the Book of Prayer route.

### Yazīd al-Faqīr
- Full name and nasab: Yazīd ibn Ṣuhayb al-Kūfī
- Kunya: Abū ʿUthmān
- Laqab / title: al-Faqīr (fatḥah on the fāʾ, then qāf) — named for pain in the vertebrae (faqār) of his back, not for poverty
- City / region: Kufa
- Tabaqa: fourth generation (Taqrīb)
- Taqrib grading line: "trustworthy" [ثقة, added]
- Critics' statements: Ibn Maʿīn, Abū Zurʿah, al-Nasāʾī — trustworthy — via Ibn Ḥajar; Abū Ḥātim, Ibn Khirāsh — truthful (ṣadūq); Ibn Khirāsh added "eminent, his narrations rare"; Ibn Ḥibbān — in al-Thiqāt — via Ibn Ḥajar
- Teachers mentioned: Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh (heard from him) — al-Maqdisī
- Students mentioned: Sayyār Abū al-Ḥakam (both Ṣaḥīḥs); Qays ibn Salīm, Abū ʿĀṣim Muḥammad ibn Ayyūb (Muslim) — al-Maqdisī
- Born / died: death year "has not been clearly identified"
- Rumuz: the six except al-Tirmidhī
- Notable: misleading laqab (Nukhbat al-Fikar category); identified inside the isnad as "he is Ibn Ṣuhayb al-Faqīr".

### Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh (Companion)
- Full name and nasab: Jābir ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn Ḥarām al-Anṣārī al-Sulamī (Anṣārī pronunciation: fatḥah on both letters, Salamī; one dialect allows kasrah on the lām)
- Kunya: Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, or Abū ʿAbdullāh, or Abū Muḥammad (al-Khazrajī)
- Nisba(s): al-Anṣārī, al-Sulamī, al-Madanī
- City / region: Madinah
- Companion facts: Companion and son of a Companion; participated in nineteen expeditions
- Hadith counts: 1,540 total (al-Khazrajī, al-Khulāṣah); agreed by both 58; al-Bukhārī alone 26; Muslim alone 126; Ibn Ḥajar (introduction to Fatḥ): 90 in al-Bukhārī
- Died: in Madinah after 70 AH, aged 94 (Taqrīb)
- Rumuz: all six

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
- al-Bukhārī joins two shaykhs with ح; he does this rarely, Muslim often, because al-Bukhārī spreads one hadith over chapters with different chains while Muslim gathers routes in one place. [scholar]
- The two shaykhs are not combined in one isnad because al-Bukhārī heard it from them separately: "ḥaddathanā" (plural) from Muḥammad ibn Sinān, "ḥaddathanī" (singular) from Saʿīd (Ibn Ḥajar, Fatḥ). [computable: formula detection; scholar: the inference]
- Muḥammad ibn Sinān says "ḥaddathanā" from Hushaym (heard his speech); Saʿīd says "akhbaranā" (read to him or heard it read) — distinctions by technical convention (Ibn Ḥajar). [computable: formula; scholar: inference]
- The lafz is Saʿīd's; the teacher observes that when al-Bukhārī narrates from several shaykhs the wording follows the last one mentioned. [scholar]
- All narrators of both routes are used by the six compilers except Yazīd al-Faqīr (not al-Tirmidhī); Muḥammad ibn Sinān not used by Muslim or al-Nasāʾī; Saʿīd ibn al-Naḍr used by al-Bukhārī only. [computable: rumuz]
- Two "misleading attributions" (Nukhbat al-Fikar): al-ʿAwqī (residence among al-ʿAwqah, not descent) and al-Faqīr (back pain, not poverty). [scholar; computable only with a nisba-origin field]
- Two narrators from Wāsiṭ: Hushaym and his shaykh Sayyār Abū al-Ḥakam. [computable: region]
- Hushaym is a known mudallis but states transmission explicitly here; everything of his in al-Bukhārī has explicit hearing (Ibn Ḥajar). [computable: tadlis flag + formula]
- Two narrators share the written nisba al-Sulamī with different vocalisation: Jābir (Anṣārī, fatḥah) and Hushaym (Banū Sulaym, ḍammah) — Ibn Ḥajar (introduction to Fatḥ), al-Nawawī (al-Taqrīb). [computable: shared nisba string; scholar: vocalisation]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - al-ruʿb — fear and dread
  - masjid — place of prostration; excludes known impure places and prohibited sites such as graveyards
  - ṭahūr (fatḥah on the ṭāʾ) — that which purifies others; "merely pure" would establish no distinction
  - maghānim — property taken from disbelievers in warfare
  - "rajul" (man) — does not exclude women (parallel: "except a man who habitually fasts")
  - "al-nās" (people) — all humans from his mission to the Day of Resurrection; includes jinn ("I was sent to all creation")
- Commentaries cited: Fatḥ al-Bārī (occasion not stated in hadith; seventeen distinctive qualities, more possible; reconciliation of "five" by progressive revelation; each quality exclusive, Nūḥ's post-Flood scope circumstantial; awe; views on "earth as masjid"; ṭahūr; tayammum with all earth); Aḥmad's Musnad (Tabūk, "Tonight I was given five"); al-ʿIrāqī (awe cast a month ahead); al-Khaṭṭābī (prior nations prayed only in designated places; prior nations' spoils were burnt by fire — in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī); al-Bazzār (Ibn ʿAbbās: no Prophet prayed until reaching his sanctuary); al-Qurṭubī (mention of dust specifies an instance without negating generality); Abū Saʿīd al-Nīsābūrī, Sharaf al-Muṣṭafā (sixty qualities); al-Suyūṭī, Anmūdhaj al-Labīb fī Khaṣāʾiṣ al-Ḥabīb (more than a thousand); Abū Umāmah narration (earth a purifier and place of prayer); Ibn ʿAbbās narration on intercession reserved for the Ummah; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim ("Every Prophet has a supplication...")
- Qur'an cited: Sabaʾ 34:28; al-Aʿrāf 7:158; al-Furqān 25:1
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Allah gave His Prophet ﷺ distinctive qualities.
  2. Superiority of Muḥammad ﷺ over the other Prophets and Messengers.
  3. Permissible to speak of the blessings Allah has granted one.
  4. Allah granted him victory by awe at a month's distance.
  5. Victory is in Allah's Hand, even without apparent means.
  6. The earth is pure by default; prayer valid on it except where impurity is certain.
  7. Ṭahūr means that which purifies others.
  8. Tayammum removes ritual impurity like water.
  9. Tayammum is permissible with all parts of the earth.
  10. Spoils of war are lawful for this Ummah.
  11. Affirmation of the Prophet's intercession.
  12. Universality of his message.
  13. Allah has made matters easy for this Ummah.
  14. Obligation to believe in unseen matters he conveyed, past and future.
  15. Prayer must not be delayed beyond its time.
  16. Honour of the human being, created from water and earth, both purifiers.
  17. Prayer and purification were legislated in principle for earlier nations.
