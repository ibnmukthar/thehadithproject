# Sahih al-Bukhari 6022

> **Teacher's numbering:** "Hadith 10" of his *Study of Sahih al-Bukhari* series (Google Doc "Hadith Ten", ID `1-f7DNkanwSohZVd56G-RkupXisWMctNwT3PnfG7c9qo`). Unlike the other documents, these notes give reference numbers themselves — al-Bukhari 6022 (Kitab al-Adab) and 1445 (Kitab al-Zakah), Muslim 1008a/1008b, al-Nasa'i 2538, al-Tayalisi 497, al-Adab al-Mufrad 225 — which match the Fath al-Bari / sunnah.com numbering.

## Text
- Arabic matn: not given in the notes (English only). [added, verify against sunnah.com bukhari:6022] «عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ صَدَقَةٌ». قَالُوا: فَإِنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ؟ قَالَ: «فَيَعْمَلُ بِيَدَيْهِ فَيَنْفَعُ نَفْسَهُ وَيَتَصَدَّقُ». قَالُوا: فَإِنْ لَمْ يَسْتَطِعْ أَوْ لَمْ يَفْعَلْ؟ قَالَ: «فَيُعِينُ ذَا الْحَاجَةِ الْمَلْهُوفَ». قَالُوا: فَإِنْ لَمْ يَفْعَلْ؟ قَالَ: «فَيَأْمُرُ بِالْخَيْرِ» أَوْ قَالَ: «بِالْمَعْرُوفِ». قَالَ: فَإِنْ لَمْ يَفْعَلْ؟ قَالَ: «فَيُمْسِكُ عَنِ الشَّرِّ، فَإِنَّهُ لَهُ صَدَقَةٌ»
- English as rendered: "Every Muslim must give charity." — "What if he has nothing to give?" — "He should work with his hands, thereby benefiting himself and giving something in charity." — "What if he is unable to do so, or does not do so?" — "He should assist someone who is distressed and in need." — "What if he does not do so?" — "He should enjoin what is good." — "What if he does not do so?" — "He should refrain from evil, for that will be an act of charity for him."
- Gist: charity is required of every Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ widens it step by step — earning and giving, helping the distressed, enjoining good, and finally refraining from evil.
- Kitab / bab: Kitab al-Adab, chapter "Every Good Deed Is Charity" (كل معروف صدقة), no. 6022

## Chain (as in al-Bukhari, compiler down to the Prophet ﷺ)
1. Adam [ibn Abi Iyas] | ḥaddathanā ("narrated to us") | al-ʿAsqalani; named bare because he is the only Adam among al-Bukhārī's teachers; not used by Muslim or Ibn Majah
2. Shuʿbah [ibn al-Hajjaj] | ḥaddathanā | Wasiti settled in Basra; named bare because he is the only Shuʿbah in the Sahih
3. Saʿid ibn Abi Burdah ibn Abi Musa al-Ashʿari | ḥaddathanā | Kufan
4. his father, Abu Burdah | ʿan abihi | Kufan; biography in the teacher's Hadith Five (not in this document)
5. his grandfather, Abu Musa al-Ashʿari | ʿan jaddihi — "who said: The Prophet ﷺ said" | Companion; Kufan; biography in the teacher's Hadith Five. "His grandfather" is Saʿid's grandfather (Abu Burdah's father); Saʿid's narration directly from his grandfather is disconnected (Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Marasil), so the link runs through his father — al-Nasa'i's route has Saʿid: "I heard my father narrating from Abu Musa"

## Narrators

### Adam ibn Abi Iyas
- Full name and nasab: Adam ibn Abi Iyas; his father's name was ʿAbd al-Rahman (Taqrīb); al-Maqdisi: ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hasan
- Kunya: Abu al-Hasan
- Nisba(s): al-ʿAsqalani; originally from Khurasan
- Mawla status: "the freedman of Taym or Tamim" (al-Maqdisi)
- City / region: grew up in Baghdad; travelled for hadith; settled in ʿAsqalan and remained there until his death (Tahdhib)
- Tabaqa: ninth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): trustworthy and devoted to worship; ninth generation; died 221 AH
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib):
  - Abu Dawud — trustworthy
  - Ahmad — "held an established position with Shuʿbah"; "one of the six or seven scholars who accurately preserved Shuʿbah's hadith"
  - Ibn Maʿin — trustworthy, although he sometimes narrated from weak narrators
  - Abu Hatim — trustworthy, dependable, devoted to worship, "among the finest worshippers of Allah"
  - Ibn Saʿd — heard a great deal from Shuʿbah
  - al-ʿIjli — trustworthy
  - Ibn Hibban — in al-Thiqat
- Teachers mentioned: Shuʿbah, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, al-Layth, Shayban ibn ʿAbd al-Rahman, Isra'il ibn Yunus, Hafs ibn Maysarah (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: al-Bukhari, "in several places"
- Died: 221 AH
- Rumuz: the canonical collections apart from Sahih Muslim and Sunan Ibn Majah
- Notable: the only Adam among al-Bukhārī's teachers. The only other Adam in Sahih al-Bukhari is Adam ibn ʿAli al-ʿIjli (a Tabiʿi; al-Bukhari and al-Nasa'i). Muslim alone has Adam ibn Sulayman al-Qurashi al-Kufi — one hadith, in Kitab al-Iman, on the last two ayahs of al-Baqarah (also in al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i). These three are all the narrators named Adam in the six books.

### Shuʿbah ibn al-Hajjaj
- Full name and nasab: Shuʿbah ibn al-Hajjaj ibn al-Ward al-ʿAtaki al-Azdi (Tahdhib)
- Kunya: Abu Bistam
- Laqab / title: "Amir al-Mu'minin fi al-hadith" (al-Thawri, reported by Ibn Mahdi); "a nation unto himself in this field" (Ahmad)
- Nisba(s): al-ʿAtaki al-Azdi; al-Wasiti
- Mawla status: "their freedman" (of al-ʿAtik / al-Azd)
- City / region: Wasit, then settled in Basra
- Tabaqa: seventh (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): trustworthy, hadith master, highly precise, devoted to worship; seventh generation; died 160 AH
- Critics' statements (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib):
  - Ahmad — "Shuʿbah was a nation unto himself in this field" (knowledge of narrators, insight, precision, selection)
  - al-Thawri (via Ibn Mahdi) — "Shuʿbah is the Commander of the Believers in hadith"
  - Wakiʿ — hoped Allah would raise him to lofty ranks in Paradise "because of his defence of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ"
  - Ibn Saʿd — trustworthy, dependable, precise, an authoritative scholar of hadith
  - Salih Jazarah — the first to speak critically about narrators was Shuʿbah, followed by al-Qattan, then Ahmad and Yahya
  - Abu Bakr ibn Manjawayh — among the leading figures of his time in memorisation, precision, piety and virtue; the first in Iraq to investigate the condition of narrators; avoided weak and abandoned narrators; the scholars of Iraq followed his approach
  - al-Hakim — the imam of the critics in Basra; saw the Companions Anas ibn Malik and ʿAmr ibn Salamah; heard from four hundred of the Tabiʿin
- Teachers mentioned: "approximately five pages" in Tahdhib (not listed in notes)
- Students mentioned: many (not listed); from the takhrij routes: Adam, Muslim ibn Ibrahim, Abu Usamah, ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Khalid
- Born / died: born 83 AH; died at the beginning of 160 AH, aged 77 (al-Maqdisi); ʿAli ibn al-Madini: ten years older than Sufyan al-Thawri
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: the only narrator named Shuʿbah in Sahih al-Bukhari

### Saʿid ibn Abi Burdah ibn Abi Musa al-Ashʿari
- Full name and nasab: Saʿid ibn Abi Burdah ibn Abi Musa al-Ashʿari al-Kufi
- Kunya: not stated
- Nisba(s): al-Ashʿari; al-Kufi
- City / region: Kufah
- Tabaqa: fifth (Taqrīb)
- Taqrīb line (as rendered): "trustworthy and firmly established. His narration from Ibn ʿUmar is mursal. He belonged to the fifth generation"
- Critics' statements:
  - Ibn Maʿin, al-ʿIjli, Abu Hatim, al-Nasa'i — trustworthy — Tahdhib al-Tahdhib
  - Ibn Hibban — in al-Thiqat — Tahdhib
  - Ibn Abi Hatim — heard nothing directly from Ibn ʿUmar (narrated from him through his father); his narration directly from his grandfather is disconnected, as he heard nothing directly from him — al-Marasil
- Teachers mentioned: his father (both Sahihs); Anas ibn Malik (Sahih Muslim) (al-Maqdisi)
- Students mentioned: Shuʿbah (both Sahihs); Abu Ishaq al-Shaybani (Sahih al-Bukhari); ʿAmr ibn Dinar, Zayd ibn Abi Unaysah, Zakariyya ibn Abi Za'idah, Mujammaʿ ibn Yahya, Qatadah (Sahih Muslim) (al-Maqdisi)
- Died: al-Sarifini: 168 AH, "as it appears in the handwriting of Mughlatay"; Ibn Ḥajar: "it may have been 'thirty' in place of 'sixty', making the year 138 AH" (Tahdhib)
- Rumuz: all six
- Notable: mursal from Ibn ʿUmar; no direct hearing from his grandfather

## Subtleties of the chain (lata'if al-isnad)
1. All five narrators are used by the six compilers except Adam ibn Abi Iyas, from whom Muslim and Ibn Majah did not narrate. [computable]
2. Three Kufans: Abu Musa, his son Abu Burdah, and his grandson Saʿid ibn Abi Burdah. [computable]
3. A son narrating from his father from his grandfather (riwāyat al-abnāʾ ʿan al-ābāʾ ʿan al-ajdād); Ibn Ḥajar (commentary on Nukhbat al-Fikar): al-Hafiz Salah al-Din al-ʿAla'i compiled a large volume on those who narrated from their fathers, from their grandfathers, from the Prophet ﷺ; Ibn Ḥajar abridged it and added many biographies; the longest such chain runs through fourteen fathers in succession. [computable]
4. "Adam" is named bare because he is the only Adam among al-Bukhārī's teachers; the three Adams of the six books are Adam ibn Abi Iyas, Adam ibn ʿAli al-ʿIjli (al-Bukhari, al-Nasa'i) and Adam ibn Sulayman al-Qurashi al-Kufi (Muslim once, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i). [computable from a name index]
5. "Shuʿbah" is named bare because no other Shuʿbah appears in Sahih al-Bukhari. [computable]
6. The chain contains an "Amir al-Mu'minin fi al-hadith": Shuʿbah ibn al-Hajjaj, so called by Sufyan al-Thawri (Tahdhib al-Tahdhib). [computable title]

## Explanation (sharh)
- Vocabulary notes:
  - "Every Muslim must give charity" — al-Tayalisi's narration specifies "every day"; Ibn Ḥajar (Fath al-Bari): either a highly emphasised recommendation or a broader meaning; the wording can indicate obligation or recommendation, as in "A Muslim owes six duties to another Muslim", which includes unanimously recommended matters
  - "What if he has nothing?" — they understood "charity" as a material gift; the Prophet ﷺ then widened it (Ibn Ḥajar)
  - الملهوف — one urgently seeking help, through helplessness or oppression; helped by action, speech or both
  - "He should enjoin what is good — or he said — what is right" — includes forbidding wrong, since the two are closely connected; both appear in al-Tayalisi's wording
  - "refrain from evil" — evil is everything the Shariʿah prohibits; the masculine pronoun in فإنه refers to the act of refraining; another narration has the feminine (فإنها), referring to the good deed of refraining; "for him" is the Muslim who refrains
- Commentaries cited: Fath al-Bari, including al-Zayn ibn al-Munayyir — the reward for refraining comes when one intends nearness to Allah by it, mere abstention has a different ruling; withholding one's harm from another is charity to that person, and from oneself is charity to oneself; the repeated "what if he has nothing?" does not establish a required sequence but shows what remains available when one deed is beyond reach — whoever can do all of them should
- Lessons / rulings as stated:
  1. Encouragement to give charity.
  2. "Charity" applies to spending wealth and to every form of good deed.
  3. The importance of working and earning to benefit oneself and others.
  4. Encouragement to perform every good deed within one's ability.
  5. When one good deed is difficult, proceed to another.
  6. Good deeds carry rewards like charity, especially for one lacking means.
  7. For one who has means, giving charity is superior to deeds whose benefit is confined to himself.
  8. Comfort for one unable to perform recommended deeds through no fault of his own.
  9. Rulings are based on what is usual — some Muslims receive the charity others are told to give.
  10. A scholar may be asked to explain a general statement and clarify its scope.
  11. The virtue of earning a living with one's hands — it preserves from the humiliation of asking and enables benefiting others.
  12. Begin with oneself and prioritise the most important ("benefiting himself and giving charity").
  13. Every good word and deed is recorded as charity.
  14. Charity in its broad sense is available to people of every financial position.
  15. Good deeds vary in merit and priority.
  16. Encouragement to assist those in need, especially the distressed.
  17. Encouragement to enjoin right and forbid wrong.
  18. Deliberate refraining is itself a rewarded deed when done for Allah.
  19. Kindness and compassion to creation are emphasised — through wealth, action, speech or abstention.
  20. The Companions' eagerness to know the truth and the levels of good deeds.
  21. Restraint from evil is charity to oneself, or to others and oneself.
  22. Refraining from evil is the minimum expected of a Muslim who performs no further good.
